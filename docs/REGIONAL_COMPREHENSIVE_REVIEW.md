# Regional & Procedures: Comprehensive Review

Prepared October 4, 2026. This proposal expands the existing block references using the Interscalene format selected by the owner. Clinical-content approval and permission to push to GitHub are still pending; this is not an installed TestFlight update.

## Scope

The catalog remains at 22 references. Fourteen block references use the comprehensive reader, while the eight existing safety and planning references remain unchanged.

- **Upper extremity:** Interscalene, supraclavicular, infraclavicular, and axillary.
- **Lower extremity and hip:** Femoral, adductor canal, iPACK, popliteal sciatic, and PENG.
- **Fascial-plane:** TAP, quadratus lumborum, and erector spinae plane.
- **Neuraxial:** Spinal and epidural.

The thirteen newly expanded topics each include an always-visible clinical summary and nine detailed sections. Interscalene retains its summary and twelve detailed sections from the earlier prototype. No new block types have been added in this batch.

## Reading experience

- **Clinical summary:** Intended use, important coverage gap, principal trade-off, and recovery priority appear first.
- **Expandable detail:** Selection, anatomy, equipment/positioning, technique safeguards, medication context, assessment/troubleshooting, complications, recovery/catheters, and evidence.
- **Quick navigation:** Anatomy, technique, medications, troubleshooting, urgent concerns, catheter/recovery, and evidence shortcuts target each block's actual sections.
- **Search:** Full-text search within detailed sections automatically opens matching sections, with a clear no-results state and reset control.
- **Navigation and access:** Existing Home button, related references, homepage search, and production subscription gate are preserved.
- **Offline boundary:** Text is bundled with the app; external evidence and ultrasound atlases require internet. No publisher ultrasound images are copied into the app.

## Block-specific clinical distinctions

- **Supraclavicular:** The reference distinguishes distal-arm coverage from shoulder and intercostobrachial requirements, and does not promise absence of phrenic or pleural risk ([NYSORA atlas](https://www.nysora.com/regional-anesthesia/techniques/ultrasound-guided-supraclavicular-brachial-plexus-block/), [2025 diaphragm systematic review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40494113/)).
- **Infraclavicular:** Variable cord position, deep vessel/pleural relationships, spread assessment, catheter function, and dose accounting are treated explicitly ([NYSORA atlas](https://www.nysora.com/regional-anesthesia/techniques/ultrasound-guided-infraclavicular-brachial-plexus-block/)).
- **Axillary:** Musculocutaneous coverage is addressed separately, with attention to compressible veins and incomplete terminal-nerve distribution ([NYSORA atlas](https://www.nysora.com/regional-anesthesia/techniques/ultrasound-guided-axillary-brachial-plexus-block/)).
- **Femoral:** Quadriceps weakness, incomplete posterior-knee/hip coverage, and assisted mobilization are emphasized ([NYSORA atlas](https://www.nysora.com/regional-anesthesia/techniques/ultrasound-guided-femoral-nerve-block/)).
- **Adductor canal:** Femoral-triangle versus canal level, proximal spread, and nonzero motor impairment are distinguished ([NYSORA atlas](https://www.nysora.com/regional-anesthesia/techniques/ultrasound-guided-saphenous-subsartorius-adductor-canal-nerve-block/)).
- **iPACK:** The adjunct role and potential foot drop remain explicit; the small study's 2-of-30 observation is not presented as a universal incidence estimate ([NYSORA technique discussion](https://nysora.com/education-news/tips-for-the-ipack-block/), [motor-outcomes study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9373452/)).
- **Popliteal sciatic:** The saphenous gap, distinction between paraneural and intraneural targets, motor recovery, catheter follow-up, and formulation-specific label context are included ([NYSORA atlas](https://www.nysora.com/regional-anesthesia/techniques/ultrasound-guided-popliteal-sciatic-block/), [EXPAREL prescribing information](https://www.exparel.com/prescribing-information.pdf)).
- **TAP and QL:** Approach-dependent coverage, somatic versus uncertain visceral benefit, cumulative bilateral dose, organ/vascular proximity, and potential weakness are not reduced to a single generic plane-block recipe ([NYSORA TAP/QL reference](https://www.nysora.com/regional-anesthesia/techniques/ultrasound-guided-transversus-abdominis-plane-quadratus-lumborum-blocks/)).
- **ESP:** Variable spread and coverage, transverse-process orientation, and limits of comparative evidence remain explicit ([NYSORA atlas](https://www.nysora.com/regional-anesthesia/techniques/erector-spinae-plane-block/), [2025 thoracic-wall review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12822337/)).
- **PENG:** Anterior-capsule analgesia is not equated with complete hip anesthesia or guaranteed motor sparing; the 2022 scoping review is identified as foundational, not a new definitive trial ([PENG review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9408030/)).
- **Epidural:** Catheter confirmation, limited test-dose reassurance, patchy/sacral-sparing block, failed surgical conversion, incremental dosing, and removal timing receive neuraxial-specific treatment ([NYSORA epidural reference](https://www.nysora.com/regional-anesthesia/techniques/epidural-anesthesia-analgesia/), [ASRA antithrombotic guideline](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/early/2025/01/21/rapm-2024-105766)).
- **Spinal:** CSF confirmation, baricity/spread, uncertain prior delivery, repeat-block risk, high block, and recovery are separated from epidural practice ([NYSORA spinal reference](https://nysora.com/regional-anesthesia/techniques/spinal-anesthesia-2/)).

## Shared safety and evidence boundaries

These are references for trained clinicians, not procedural checklists, automated dosing tools, or substitutes for supervised training. All clinical content still requires the owner's review before publication.

- **Dose context:** Published atlas volumes are labeled as context, not default orders or safe maximums. Bilateral and combined-block exposure must be considered together, and liposomal-bupivacaine indications are not extrapolated to other sites ([EXPAREL prescribing information](https://www.exparel.com/prescribing-information.pdf)).
- **Bleeding and infection:** Drug/dose, renal function, needle placement, catheter removal, and restart decisions remain linked to the complete guidance; no new generic hold-time shortcut was introduced ([ASRA fifth-edition antithrombotic guideline](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/early/2025/01/21/rapm-2024-105766), [ASRA infection-control guideline](https://doi.org/10.1136/rapm-2024-105651)).
- **Neurologic and systemic toxicity:** Unexpected or progressive deficits require evaluation rather than attribution to an intended motor-sparing technique, and suspected systemic toxicity links to the rescue aid ([ASRA neurologic advisory](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/rapm/40/5/401.full.pdf), [ASRA LAST aid](https://asra.com/docs/default-source/guidelines-articles/local-anesthetic-systemic-toxicity-rgb.pdf?sfvrsn=33b348e_2)).
- **Evidence dates:** A recent review publication does not mean its search includes the newest trials; the 2025 diaphragm review's December 2022 search boundary and the heterogeneity of rebound-pain evidence are retained ([diaphragm review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40494113/), [2026 rebound-pain meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13446866/)).
- **Original synthesis:** No Vargo prose, screenshots, or illustrations are embedded. Linked publisher atlases remain external.
