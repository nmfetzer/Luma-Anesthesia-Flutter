## AICDs & Pacemakers

### Critical distinctions

Content version: 2026-09-27. Section ID: `cied-critical`.

Adult perioperative clinical reference. Individualize with the anesthesia/CIED team, exact-model instructions, and institutional policy; not a patient-specific device prescription.

- **Pacemaker magnet:** Often produces asynchronous pacing, but the response can be disabled, temporary, absent, or unsuitable for the patient; verify the actual response before relying on it. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))
- **ICD/AICD magnet:** Generally suspends tachyarrhythmia treatment, but does not convert the device to asynchronous pacing or protect a pacing-dependent patient from EMI-related pacing inhibition. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))
- **Pacing-dependent ICD/CRT-D:** When clinically significant electromagnetic interference (EMI) is anticipated, plan asynchronous pacing by reprogramming and separately suspend tachyarrhythmia therapy; a magnet alone is not an adequate pacing plan. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))
- **Micra:** Has no magnet-response mode; use device programming when asynchronous pacing is needed. ([Medtronic magnet guidance](https://wwwp.medtronic.com/crs-upload/letters/102/102_D00874408--rev_B_Magnet-ICD.pdf))
- **Before leaving monitored care:** Restore required pacing settings and confirm that ICD tachyarrhythmia therapies are enabled; removing a magnet does not reverse programmer-based deactivation. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))

### Identify the device

Content version: 2026-09-27. Section ID: `cied-identify`.

Adult perioperative clinical reference. Individualize with the anesthesia/CIED team, exact-model instructions, and institutional policy; not a patient-specific device prescription.

- **Permanent pacemaker / PPM:** Provides bradycardia pacing; a pacing-only system does not deliver ICD shocks. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))
- **ICD / AICD:** Treats ventricular tachyarrhythmias; transvenous systems commonly also provide bradycardia pacing and antitachycardia pacing, depending on the model and settings. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))
- **CRT-P versus CRT-D:** CRT-P provides resynchronization pacing without defibrillation, whereas CRT-D combines resynchronization and ICD functions; pacing and defibrillation management must be considered separately. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))
- **Leadless pacemaker:** An intracardiac device may be present without a palpable chest generator; identify the manufacturer and configuration rather than assuming all leadless devices behave alike. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))
- **S-ICD / EV-ICD:** Identify the specific system and any separate pacemaker; their pacing capabilities are not interchangeable with a conventional transvenous ICD. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))
- **Identification sources:** Use the implant card, interrogation/remote report, EHR and device clinic; chest radiography can help identify leads, coils, and device type when records are unavailable. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))

### Pre-op assessment and device-team plan

Content version: 2026-09-27. Section ID: `cied-preop`.

Adult perioperative clinical reference. Individualize with the anesthesia/CIED team, exact-model instructions, and institutional policy; not a patient-specific device prescription.

#### Information to obtain

- **Device details:** Type, manufacturer, model, location, implant indication, battery status, programmed mode, pacing/capture thresholds, sensing, lead integrity, and known advisories. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))
- **Pacing needs:** Underlying rhythm, rate, pacing burden, dependence, and whether losing AV synchrony or resynchronization would compromise hemodynamics. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/); [magnet-management review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8836758/))
- **Magnet details:** Programmed response, observed response, expected rate, any timeout or delay, how to confirm activation, and whether the magnet can remain accessible and stable after positioning/draping. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))
- **Clinical change:** New syncope, worsening heart failure, recent ICD shocks/ATP, high arrhythmia burden, or suspected device malfunction warrants further assessment before elective care. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))
- **Procedure details:** Urgency, operative site, monopolar versus bipolar energy, expected current path, patient position, generator accessibility, and planned recovery location. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))
- **Ownership:** Document who will program the device, verify the perioperative response, restore settings, and confirm completion before discharge from monitored care. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))

#### How recent should interrogation be?

- **AHA approach:** An interrogation from within approximately 3 months may be reviewed to assess recent activity and pacing burden; this is not a blanket rule that every patient needs a new same-day interrogation. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))
- **ASA context:** The 2020 advisory reports agreement around interrogation within 3–6 months, while noting insufficient evidence for one optimal interval; an adequate remote report can provide needed information. ([ASA 2020](https://www.swa10.com/uploads/6/1/4/3/61438899/aicd_asa_2020.pdf))
- **Clinical changes override the calendar:** Reassess if proper function is uncertain, symptoms have changed, or the available report cannot support the planned procedure. ([ASA 2020](https://www.swa10.com/uploads/6/1/4/3/61438899/aicd_asa_2020.pdf))

#### Pacing dependence

- **Meaning:** Dependence includes an absent intrinsic rhythm or an intrinsic rhythm inadequate for the patient's needs; pacing percentage alone is not a complete clinical assessment. ([ASA 2020](https://www.swa10.com/uploads/6/1/4/3/61438899/aicd_asa_2020.pdf); [AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))
- **Anesthesia effect:** A patient with an adequate awake rhythm may become dependent or functionally inadequate with anesthesia, altered autonomic tone, or medications. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))
- **Assessment method:** Review the ECG and interrogation with the device team; formal testing of an underlying rhythm requires appropriate programming expertise and monitoring, not an unmonitored bedside experiment. ([ASA 2020](https://www.swa10.com/uploads/6/1/4/3/61438899/aicd_asa_2020.pdf))

### Pacemaker magnet protocol

Content version: 2026-09-27. Section ID: `cied-pacemaker-magnet`.

Adult perioperative clinical reference. Individualize with the anesthesia/CIED team, exact-model instructions, and institutional policy; not a patient-specific device prescription.

- **Confirm suitability:** Establish that the specific device produces a sustained, appropriate asynchronous response and that the magnet rate and chamber configuration meet the patient's needs. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))
- **Test under monitoring:** Assess ECG response and a mechanical pulse signal before relying on the magnet; secure it in the manufacturer-recommended position and reassess after positioning. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))
- **Understand the modes:** AOO is asynchronous atrial pacing, VOO asynchronous ventricular pacing, and DOO asynchronous dual-chamber pacing; the correct choice depends on the patient's rhythm and device system. ([ASA 2020](https://www.swa10.com/uploads/6/1/4/3/61438899/aicd_asa_2020.pdf))
- **Prefer programming when needed:** Use a programmed plan if magnet response is absent/disabled, transient, hemodynamically unsuitable, inaccessible, or cannot be continuously maintained outside the sterile field. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))
- **Avoid routine asynchronous pacing without an indication:** Competition with an intrinsic rhythm can be proarrhythmic or hemodynamically unfavorable; “more pacing” is not automatically safer. ([Magnet-management review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8836758/))
- **Watch CRT behavior:** Magnet mode can change AV/VV timing or resynchronization in some CRT-P systems; do not assume that seeing paced beats means the patient's usual resynchronization is preserved. ([Magnet-management review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8836758/))

### ICD/AICD and CRT-D magnet protocol

Content version: 2026-09-27. Section ID: `cied-icd-magnet`.

Adult perioperative clinical reference. Individualize with the anesthesia/CIED team, exact-model instructions, and institutional policy; not a patient-specific device prescription.

- **Define the goal:** Suspend inappropriate tachyarrhythmia treatment from EMI; a magnet is not a substitute for asynchronous programming when pacing protection is required. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))
- **Prepare rescue first:** Ensure a monitored environment, external defibrillation/pacing capability, and appropriately positioned external pads while ICD therapies are disabled. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))
- **Confirm the device-specific response:** Verify that magnet response is enabled and that the intended therapy suspension occurs; tones and their absence are model-dependent. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))
- **Maintain placement:** Keep the magnet continuously in the correct position throughout the period requiring therapy suspension; prone/lateral positioning, deep implants, or a nearby sterile field may favor reprogramming. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))
- **Separate treatment from detection:** Boston Scientific transvenous ICDs may continue detecting/recording arrhythmias while magnet application inhibits ATP and shocks; do not label all magnet responses as “detection off.” ([Boston Scientific](https://www.bostonscientific.com/content/dam/bostonscientific/quality/education-resources/english/US_ACL_Magnet%20Use%20with%20BSC%20CIED_20210421.pdf))
- **Confirm recovery:** Magnet removal usually restores the prior treatment state, but model-specific delays and older-device exceptions exist; explicitly verify restoration rather than assuming every system reactivates immediately. ([ASA 2020](https://www.swa10.com/uploads/6/1/4/3/61438899/aicd_asa_2020.pdf); [magnet-management review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8836758/))

Related section: **External Defibrillation & Cardioversion** in this guide covers emergency shocks, pad placement, and post-shock device checks.

#### Leadless devices

- **Micra VR/AV:** No magnet response; a dependent patient requiring EMI protection needs a programmed asynchronous plan rather than an external magnet. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))
- **AVEIR:** Magnet response depends on configuration: ventricular-only VOO, atrial-only AOO, and dual-chamber DOO as described by AHA; body habitus and intracardiac location can make reliable activation difficult. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))
- **AVEIR VR model LSP112V:** The manufacturer manual specifies, before RRT and with response enabled, 100 bpm for 5 cycles followed by a battery-dependent rate; “Off” disables the response. ([Abbott AVEIR manual](https://manuals.eifu.abbott/content/dam/av/manuals-eifu/global/IS/en/ARTEN600186860_A.pdf))
- **Clinical implication:** Do not rely on a leadless magnet plan unless actual activation and sustained capture are demonstrated; use programming when the response is unreliable. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))

#### Newer technical finding: verify response after interrogation

- **2025 bench study:** In 23 tested devices, seven Biotronik ICDs did not enter magnet inhibition immediately after interrogation, with observed delays of 5–7 minutes; two tested Medtronic pacemakers showed approximately 1.5-minute delays. ([Kreimer et al., 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12692141/))
- **Evidence limit:** These were laboratory findings in selected models, not a universal waiting-time protocol or a reason to omit indicated interrogation; the practical safeguard is model-specific verification before EMI exposure. ([Kreimer et al., 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12692141/))

### Electrocautery and EMI precautions

Content version: 2026-09-27. Section ID: `cied-electrocautery`.

Adult perioperative clinical reference. Individualize with the anesthesia/CIED team, exact-model instructions, and institutional policy; not a patient-specific device prescription.

- **Energy choice:** Prefer bipolar electrosurgery or an ultrasonic device when appropriate; bipolar energy still should not be applied directly to the CIED system. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/); [ASA 2020](https://www.swa10.com/uploads/6/1/4/3/61438899/aicd_asa_2020.pdf))
- **Monopolar technique:** Use the lowest effective energy and short, intermittent bursts, generally no longer than 5 seconds, with pauses when feasible. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/); [ASA 2020](https://www.swa10.com/uploads/6/1/4/3/61438899/aicd_asa_2020.pdf))
- **Return electrode:** Choose placement so current travels away from, not through or near, the generator and leads; a contralateral lower-limb site may help depending on the operative field. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))
- **Below-umbilicus cases:** The lower-risk description assumes the return pad is also below the umbilicus and the device/current path does not create another hazard; an abdominal generator requires a separate assessment. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))
- **Whole-body return electrodes:** Avoid them in the AHA risk-mitigation approach because they can undermine assumptions about a current path remote from the device. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))
- **Other EMI procedures:** RF ablation and endoscopic monopolar/argon plasma techniques also require device planning; “not open surgery” does not mean no EMI risk. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))

### Intraoperative monitoring and troubleshooting

Content version: 2026-09-27. Section ID: `cied-intraop`.

Adult perioperative clinical reference. Individualize with the anesthesia/CIED team, exact-model instructions, and institutional policy; not a patient-specific device prescription.

#### Monitoring and preparation

- **Rhythm plus pulse:** Monitor ECG and a mechanical pulse signal, such as pulse-oximetry plethysmography or an arterial waveform when clinically indicated; cautery can obscure ECG and displayed pacing spikes are not proof of effective capture. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))
- **Rescue equipment:** Ensure immediate external pacing and defibrillation capability, with pads applied when ICD therapies are suspended. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))
- **Pad position:** Avoid placing defibrillation pads directly over the generator; use an anterior-posterior vector when feasible and keep pads as far from the generator as practical without compromising resuscitation. ([ASA 2020](https://www.swa10.com/uploads/6/1/4/3/61438899/aicd_asa_2020.pdf))
- **Rate-responsive sensors:** Include sensor management in the device plan when procedural stimulation could cause inappropriate tachycardia; an ICD magnet does not reliably address this. ([ASA 2020](https://www.swa10.com/uploads/6/1/4/3/61438899/aicd_asa_2020.pdf); [magnet-management review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8836758/))

#### Bradycardia, inhibition, or loss of capture

- **Immediate assessment:** Stop the EMI source, assess rhythm and actual perfusion, and determine whether the problem is interference, loss of capture, or another cause of instability. ([ASA 2020](https://www.swa10.com/uploads/6/1/4/3/61438899/aicd_asa_2020.pdf))
- **Rescue pacing:** Use external pacing and resuscitation as indicated while arranging device-team assistance; use a pacemaker magnet only when its response is known and appropriate, not as a blind response to every slow rhythm. ([ASA 2020](https://www.swa10.com/uploads/6/1/4/3/61438899/aicd_asa_2020.pdf))
- **ICD limitation:** Applying a magnet to an ICD does not solve EMI-induced bradycardia pacing inhibition; address pacing through programming or external support. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))

#### Repeated shocks or true VT/VF

- **Suspected inappropriate shocks:** Stop EMI, rapidly assess the actual rhythm and pulse, and seek device-team assistance; verified magnet application can temporarily suppress inappropriate ICD therapy while external rescue capability is maintained. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/); [ASA 2020](https://www.swa10.com/uploads/6/1/4/3/61438899/aicd_asa_2020.pdf))
- **True unstable VT/VF or cardiac arrest:** Follow the appropriate resuscitation pathway without delaying indicated external cardioversion/defibrillation for a programmer, device confirmation, or an ideal pad position; remove an ICD magnet when restoring native therapy, but do not wait for internal therapy if external treatment is needed now. ([ASA 2020](https://www.swa10.com/uploads/6/1/4/3/61438899/aicd_asa_2020.pdf))
- **After rescue:** Arrange interrogation following external cardioversion/defibrillation, arrest, suspected ICD therapy, or device malfunction/reset. ([ASA 2020](https://www.swa10.com/uploads/6/1/4/3/61438899/aicd_asa_2020.pdf); [AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))

### External Defibrillation & Cardioversion

Content version: 2026-09-27. Section ID: `cied-external-defibrillation`.

Adult perioperative clinical reference. Individualize with the anesthesia/CIED team, exact-model instructions, and institutional policy; not a patient-specific device prescription.

#### Emergency priority

**Do not delay an indicated external shock because a pacemaker or ICD is present.** Follow the appropriate resuscitation pathway; do not wait for a programmer or restoration of disabled ICD therapies if they cannot be restored promptly. ([ASA practice advisory, 2020](https://www.swa10.com/uploads/6/1/4/3/61438899/aicd_asa_2020.pdf))

#### Choose the correct treatment

- **VF or pulseless VT:** Deliver unsynchronized defibrillation and immediately resume CPR according to the cardiac-arrest algorithm; an ICD that has not terminated the rhythm is not a reason to withhold external shocks. ([ERC adult ALS guidelines, 2025](https://www.erc.edu/media/vedoa2ga/gl2025-05-als-e.pdf))
- **Unstable tachyarrhythmia with a pulse:** Use synchronized cardioversion for appropriate rhythms such as monomorphic VT, SVT, or AF/flutter when the arrhythmia is causing instability; synchronize to the R wave, and if synchronization fails in unstable VT, give an unsynchronized shock rather than prolonging the delay. ([AHA adult ALS guidelines, 2025](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/adult-advanced-life-support); [ERC adult ALS guidelines, 2025](https://www.erc.edu/media/vedoa2ga/gl2025-05-als-e.pdf))
- **Sustained polymorphic VT:** Use an immediate high-energy unsynchronized shock, even if a pulse is initially present; the changing QRS complexes cannot be synchronized reliably. ([AHA adult ALS guidelines, 2025](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/adult-advanced-life-support))
- **Conscious patient:** Provide appropriate sedation/anesthesia for cardioversion when feasible, accounting for the risk of hemodynamic deterioration and the urgency of treatment. ([ERC adult ALS guidelines, 2025](https://www.erc.edu/media/vedoa2ga/gl2025-05-als-e.pdf))
- **Planned cardioversion:** This emergency device-safety reference does not replace assessment of anticoagulation and thromboembolic risk before elective cardioversion of atrial fibrillation. ([ERC adult ALS guidelines, 2025](https://www.erc.edu/media/vedoa2ga/gl2025-05-als-e.pdf))

#### Pad placement

- **Never directly over the generator:** Position pads to minimize current through the generator and leads while maintaining an effective transthoracic shock vector. ([ASA practice advisory, 2020](https://www.swa10.com/uploads/6/1/4/3/61438899/aicd_asa_2020.pdf))
- **Distance:** Aim for more than 8 cm between the pad and the implanted generator, consistent with ERC 2025 guidance; use an alternative pad position when needed and do not delay lifesaving treatment while seeking an ideal position. ([ERC adult ALS guidelines, 2025](https://www.erc.edu/media/vedoa2ga/gl2025-05-als-e.pdf))
- **Orientation:** ASA perioperative guidance favors anterior–posterior placement when feasible; anterior–lateral placement remains an alternative when it avoids the device and permits prompt effective treatment. ([ASA practice advisory, 2020](https://www.swa10.com/uploads/6/1/4/3/61438899/aicd_asa_2020.pdf); [ERC adult ALS guidelines, 2025](https://www.erc.edu/media/vedoa2ga/gl2025-05-als-e.pdf))
- **S-ICD location:** A subcutaneous ICD generator may be in the left lateral chest where a lateral pad would normally go; identify the actual generator location rather than assuming a left infraclavicular implant. ([ERC adult ALS guidelines, 2025](https://www.erc.edu/media/vedoa2ga/gl2025-05-als-e.pdf))

#### Energy selection

- **Use the indicated energy:** Follow the current ACLS rhythm-specific algorithm and external-defibrillator instructions; use clinically appropriate energy regardless of the implanted device and do not reduce it solely to protect a pacemaker or ICD. ([ASA practice advisory, 2020](https://www.swa10.com/uploads/6/1/4/3/61438899/aicd_asa_2020.pdf))

#### Magnet or disabled ICD therapies

- **Magnet-suspended ICD:** Stop sources of EMI and remove the magnet to allow native ICD tachyarrhythmia therapy to resume, observing for an appropriate response; do not wait for an internal shock when external treatment is needed immediately. ([ASA practice advisory, 2020](https://www.swa10.com/uploads/6/1/4/3/61438899/aicd_asa_2020.pdf))
- **Programmed-off ICD:** Removing a magnet does not reverse programmer-disabled therapies; arrange reprogramming, but proceed with emergency external cardioversion/defibrillation if restoration cannot occur promptly. ([ASA practice advisory, 2020](https://www.swa10.com/uploads/6/1/4/3/61438899/aicd_asa_2020.pdf))
- **Pacemaker distinction:** A pacing-only device does not provide ICD shock therapy, and an ICD magnet generally does not convert its pacing to an asynchronous mode; pacing support and tachyarrhythmia treatment remain separate considerations. ([AHA CIED scientific statement, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))

#### After external cardioversion or defibrillation

- **Immediate device assessment:** Arrange immediate CIED interrogation following external cardioversion/defibrillation, without interrupting ongoing resuscitation; assess for reset, programming changes, and impaired pacing or defibrillator function. ([ASA practice advisory, 2020](https://www.swa10.com/uploads/6/1/4/3/61438899/aicd_asa_2020.pdf); [AHA CIED scientific statement, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))
- **Continue monitoring and rescue readiness:** Keep cardiac rate/rhythm monitoring and backup pacing/defibrillation available until permanent device settings are restored. ([ASA practice advisory, 2020](https://www.swa10.com/uploads/6/1/4/3/61438899/aicd_asa_2020.pdf))
- **Before leaving monitored care:** Confirm ICD tachyarrhythmia detection and therapies are re-enabled, document the device plan and changes, and communicate them in the handoff. ([AHA CIED scientific statement, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))

### Post-op restoration and handoff

Content version: 2026-09-27. Section ID: `cied-postop`.

Adult perioperative clinical reference. Individualize with the anesthesia/CIED team, exact-model instructions, and institutional policy; not a patient-specific device prescription.

- **Restore settings:** Remove any magnet when no longer required and have programmed changes reversed or replaced with an appropriate ongoing prescription by qualified personnel. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))
- **Confirm ICD protection:** Keep continuous monitoring and rescue capability until required pacing settings and ICD tachyarrhythmia therapies are restored; confirm reactivation before transfer out of monitored care. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))
- **Interrogate after significant events:** Arrest, external cardioversion/defibrillation, observed or suspected ICD treatment, suspected reset/malfunction, or substantial concerning EMI exposure warrants postprocedure device evaluation. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/); [ASA 2020](https://www.swa10.com/uploads/6/1/4/3/61438899/aicd_asa_2020.pdf))
- **Emergency procedure without adequate pre-op evaluation:** Arrange postoperative interrogation rather than allowing the missing assessment to remain unresolved. ([ASA 2020](https://www.swa10.com/uploads/6/1/4/3/61438899/aicd_asa_2020.pdf))
- **Uncomplicated low-risk case:** Immediate interrogation is not universally required after an uneventful, appropriately planned case with no concerning events; follow the device-team plan and routine follow-up when appropriate. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/); [ASA 2020](https://www.swa10.com/uploads/6/1/4/3/61438899/aicd_asa_2020.pdf))
- **Handoff content:** Device identity, dependence, programming changes, magnet use, perioperative events, restoration status, person confirming reactivation, and follow-up arrangements belong in the documented handoff. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))

### Urgent case with incomplete device information

Content version: 2026-09-27. Section ID: `cied-urgent`.

Adult perioperative clinical reference. Individualize with the anesthesia/CIED team, exact-model instructions, and institutional policy; not a patient-specific device prescription.

- **Parallel preparation:** Use available records, device card, ECG, imaging if appropriate, and rapid device-team contact while establishing continuous monitoring and external pacing/defibrillation readiness. ([ASA 2020](https://www.swa10.com/uploads/6/1/4/3/61438899/aicd_asa_2020.pdf))
- **Do not guess the magnet response:** A magnet is neither universal pacing protection nor proof that an unidentified ICD's therapies are suspended. ([AHA 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068544/))
- **Urgency-specific decision:** The treating team must balance the urgency of surgery against unresolved device risk; after emergency surgery performed without appropriate device evaluation, arrange postoperative interrogation. ([ASA 2020](https://www.swa10.com/uploads/6/1/4/3/61438899/aicd_asa_2020.pdf))

## Anaphylaxis

### Adult perioperative treatment

Content version: 2026-09-27. Section ID: `anaphylaxis-adult-perioperative-treatment`.

| Intervention | Dose / action · safety |
|---|---|
| **Recognize & act** | Sudden hypotension, bronchospasm or airway swelling may occur **without skin signs**. Stop suspected trigger, call for help, give oxygen, secure airway/ventilation and begin rapid circulatory support. ([RCUK perioperative algorithm](https://www.resus.org.uk/sites/default/files/2024-01/2526%20AAP%20RCUK%20periop%20anaphylaxis-8C.pdf)) |
| **Epinephrine: monitored IV setting** | For an experienced anesthesia/resuscitation clinician: **50 mcg IV initially**, titrate further boluses to response and start infusion for poor response. **50 mcg = 0.5 mL of 0.1 mg/mL**. This is a perioperative monitored-IV protocol, not general first-aid advice. ([RCUK](https://www.resus.org.uk/sites/default/files/2024-01/2526%20AAP%20RCUK%20periop%20anaphylaxis-8C.pdf)) |
| **No IV / IM treatment** | **10 mcg/kg IM, maximum 500 mcg**, using **1 mg/mL** epinephrine, while obtaining IV/IO access. General adult anaphylaxis guidance uses **500 mcg IM**; follow local repeat-dose protocol. **Never give the 1 mg/mL preparation undiluted as a 50 mcg IV bolus.** ([RCUK perioperative algorithm](https://www.resus.org.uk/sites/default/files/2024-01/2526%20AAP%20RCUK%20periop%20anaphylaxis-8C.pdf); [RCUK guidance](https://www.resus.org.uk/library/additional-guidance/guidance-anaphylaxis)) |
| **Epinephrine infusion** | Use local pump protocol. RCUK fallback: **0.5 mg in a final 50 mL (10 mcg/mL)**; start **0.5–1 mL/kg/h**, equivalent to approximately **0.08–0.17 mcg/kg/min**, then titrate. Continuous ECG/BP monitoring and observed IV access are mandatory. ([RCUK](https://www.resus.org.uk/sites/default/files/2024-01/2526%20AAP%20RCUK%20periop%20anaphylaxis-8C.pdf)) |
| **Fluid** | Rapid **500–1000 mL IV crystalloid**, reassess and repeat to response; several liters may be required. **Avoid colloid** in this algorithm. ([RCUK](https://www.resus.org.uk/sites/default/files/2024-01/2526%20AAP%20RCUK%20periop%20anaphylaxis-8C.pdf)) |
| **Refractory shock / beta-blockade** | Continue epinephrine/fluids; expert-guided additional norepinephrine or vasopressin may be required. Adult on beta-blocker: consider **glucagon 1 mg IV**. Involve ICU early and consider extracorporeal support for refractory collapse. ([RCUK](https://www.resus.org.uk/sites/default/files/2024-01/2526%20AAP%20RCUK%20periop%20anaphylaxis-8C.pdf)) |
| **Adjuncts are not first-line rescue** | Bronchodilators can help persistent bronchospasm. Antihistamines/steroids must **not delay epinephrine and fluids**; steroids may be considered for refractory reactions/shock, not as the initial lifesaving treatment. ([RCUK](https://www.resus.org.uk/sites/default/files/2024-01/2526%20AAP%20RCUK%20periop%20anaphylaxis-8C.pdf)) |
| **After stabilization** | Serum tryptase once stable, another sample **1–4 h after onset**, baseline **≥24 h later**. Document exposures/timing and arrange allergy investigation; plan monitored disposition based on severity. ([RCUK](https://www.resus.org.uk/sites/default/files/2024-01/2526%20AAP%20RCUK%20periop%20anaphylaxis-8C.pdf)) |

### Safety & perioperative use

Content version: 2026-09-27. Section ID: `beta-blocker-safety`.

| Check | Pertinent detail |
|---|---|
| **Scope** | Adult monitored IV quick reference, not pediatric, obstetric, thyroid-storm, overdose or outpatient oral-titration protocols. Confirm indication, concentration, local protocol and patient-specific BP/HR targets. |
| **Before dosing** | Check ECG/rhythm, BP, perfusion, volume status and ventricular function. **Do not suppress compensatory tachycardia from hypovolemia.** Monitor ECG and BP during administration; reduce/stop for clinically significant hypotension or bradyarrhythmia. ([Esmolol label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2023/019386s053lbl.pdf); [Metoprolol label](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?id=17205)) |
| **Cardiac risks** | **Do not give in cardiogenic shock, decompensated HF or severe bradycardia.** Significant AV block/sinus-node disease requires product-specific assessment; a pacemaker does not remove hypotension or negative-inotropy risk. Metoprolol's US acute-MI label also contraindicates **HR <45/min, SBP <100 mmHg or PR ≥0.24 sec**; these are label-specific thresholds, not universal treatment targets. ([Metoprolol label](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?id=17205); [Esmolol label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2023/019386s053lbl.pdf)) |
| **Pre-excited AF / WPW** | **Avoid beta-blockers and other AV-nodal blockers in pre-excited AF** because of ventricular-fibrillation/hemodynamic-deterioration risk. Do not generalize older propranolol-label references to “WPW” into permission to treat pre-excited AF. ([AF guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001193)) |
| **Unstable AF** | Hemodynamic instability attributable to AF calls for **immediate synchronized electrical cardioversion**, not repeated beta-blocker dosing. This chart is not a cardioversion or anticoagulation protocol. ([AF guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001193)) |
| **Interactions / airway** | Verapamil, diltiazem, digoxin and other rate-slowing drugs can increase bradycardia/block risk. **Beta-1 selectivity does not eliminate bronchospasm risk.** Nonselective labetalol/propranolol are contraindicated in asthma; evaluate airway disease before any beta-blocker. ([Metoprolol label](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?id=17205); [Labetalol label](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?id=4492); [Propranolol label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=f14ea537-dd5c-4c78-9037-9a85e9e610ef)) |
| **Chronic therapy / new start** | **Continue established stable beta-blocker therapy** perioperatively as clinically appropriate. For a new elective indication, initiate **optimally >7 days before surgery** to assess tolerance/titrate. **Do not newly initiate on surgery day when there is no immediate need**; this does not prohibit treatment of an acute indication. ([2024 AHA/ACC guideline recommendations](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)) |
| **Pheochromocytoma / glucose** | For pheochromocytoma, initiate appropriate **alpha blockade before beta blockade**; do not use beta blockade alone. Beta-blockers may mask hypoglycemic tachycardia and increase severe/prolonged hypoglycemia risk, especially while fasting. ([Metoprolol label](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?id=17205); [Landiolol label](https://www.rapiblyk.com/fileadmin/user_upload/MediaLibrary_Rapiblyk/Documents/Rapiblyk_Full_Prescribing_Information.pdf)) |
| **Units / formulation** | Esmolol and landiolol infusions: **mcg/kg/min**. Labetalol infusion: **mg/min**. Esmolol **500 mcg/kg = 0.5 mg/kg**. Verify actual vial/bag concentration; do not infer mL from drug name or substitute oral extended-release formulations for IV products. ([Esmolol label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2023/019386s053lbl.pdf); [Labetalol label](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?id=4492); [Landiolol label](https://www.rapiblyk.com/fileadmin/user_upload/MediaLibrary_Rapiblyk/Documents/Rapiblyk_Full_Prescribing_Information.pdf)) |

### Intraoperative treatment

| Intervention | Adult dose / action · pertinent details |
|---|---|
| **Immediate assessment** | Call for help, give oxygen, hand-ventilate and assess ventilation. Exclude **kink/obstruction, mainstem tube, circuit failure, aspiration, pulmonary edema or anaphylaxis** before attributing high airway pressure solely to bronchospasm. ## Hemodynamic Support

### Preload, afterload, inotropy & HR

Content version: 2026-09-27-audit1. Section ID: `hemodynamic-support-preload-afterload-inotropy-hr`.

| Goal / mechanism | Practical options · key caution |
|---|---|
| **Increase preload** | Assess volume responsiveness, bleeding and ventricular function. Give appropriate fluid or blood when volume is deficient; reassess rather than repeatedly bolusing a nonresponsive/congested patient. Vasoconstriction does not replace resuscitation of hemorrhage. ([Perioperative review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13413041/); [Trauma guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9977110/)) |
| **Increase afterload / SVR** | **Phenylephrine 50–100 mcg IV initially** when vasodilation and adequate HR/output are present; may reduce HR and cardiac output. **Norepinephrine 0.02–0.1 mcg/kg/min** is a perioperative reference infusion range, not a maximum. ([Phenylephrine label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2025/216830s000lbl.pdf); [Stanford](https://cvanes.stanford.edu/perioperative-vasoactives/); [APSF](https://www.apsf.org/article/perioperative-hypotension/)) |
| **Catecholamine-resistant vasoplegia** | **Vasopressin 0.01–0.04 units/min IV** is a perioperative adjunct range. May reduce cardiac output and cause coronary, mesenteric or limb ischemia; evaluate volume and pump function. ([Review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13413041/); [Label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2024/217569Orig1s001lbl.pdf)) |
| **Increase inotropy** | **Dobutamine: usual titrated range 2–20 mcg/kg/min IV**, label starting rate **0.5–1 mcg/kg/min**; or **milrinone 0.375–0.75 mcg/kg/min IV** for selected low-output states. Both can lower SVR/BP; milrinone requires renal dose reduction. Epinephrine can provide combined inotropic/pressor support; see Hypotension Dosing. ([Dobutamine label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2023/020255s025lbl.pdf); [Milrinone](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/2625/smpc)) |
| **Increase HR: transient low-HR hypotension** | **Ephedrine 5–10 mg IV**, repeat to response; **50 mg total maximum**. Tachyphylaxis and arrhythmias can occur. Do not confuse this with the unstable-bradycardia algorithm. ([Label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2024/208609s012lbl.pdf)) |
| **Increase HR: compromised bradycardia** | **Atropine 1 mg IV every 3–5 min; max 3 mg**. If ineffective: pacing and/or **epinephrine 2–10 mcg/min IV** or **dopamine 5–20 mcg/kg/min IV**. Correct reversible causes and seek expert help. ([2025 AHA](https://cpr.heart.org/-/media/CPR-Files/CPR-Guidelines-Files/2025-Algorithms/Algorithm-ACLS-Bradycardia-250514.pdf?sc_lang=en)) |
| **Reassess the result** | BP alone is not cardiac output. Recheck perfusion, rhythm, volume status and ventricular function; a pure vasoconstrictor may worsen low-output physiology. ([APSF](https://www.apsf.org/article/perioperative-hypotension/)) |

### Selection & safety chart

Content version: 2026-09-27. Section ID: `hypotension-safety`.

| Check | Pertinent detail |
|---|---|
| **Scope** | Adult, monitored perioperative reference. Not pediatric, obstetric, cardiac-arrest or anaphylaxis protocols. Shock-specific label regimens are marked separately. No automatic dose or concentration calculations. |
| **Treat the mechanism** | Assess anesthetic effect, volume/preload, HR and ventricular function; correct the cause while supporting perfusion. A higher BP does **not** guarantee adequate cardiac output. Do not substitute vasoconstriction for needed volume resuscitation. ([APSF](https://www.apsf.org/article/perioperative-hypotension/); [Review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13413041/)) |
| **Match physiology** | Vasodilation with adequate HR: phenylephrine or norepinephrine may fit. Low HR: ephedrine may fit transient hypotension. Myocardial depression: consider an agent with inotropic support rather than a pure vasoconstrictor. Catecholamine-resistant vasoplegia: vasopressin may be an adjunct. Not a substitute for evaluating the cause. ([Review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13413041/)) |
| **Dose and concentration** | Ephedrine bolus: **mg**. Catecholamine/phenylephrine boluses: **mcg**. Vasopressin: **units**. Distinguish **mcg/min** from **mcg/kg/min** and **units/min** from **units/h**. Verify syringe/bag label and pump settings; never infer an mL dose from the drug name. ([FDA ephedrine label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2024/208609s012lbl.pdf); [Stanford reference](https://cvanes.stanford.edu/perioperative-vasoactives/)) |
| **Monitoring / IV access** | Frequent or continuous BP assessment, ECG, perfusion and IV-site surveillance. Use an appropriate large vein and follow local peripheral-pressor policy; escalating/prolonged support may need central access and invasive monitoring. Titrate to individualized BP/perfusion targets. ([Norepinephrine label](https://www.dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=728734e7-1684-4b37-980c-f1dfd408b436&type=display); [Review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13413041/)) |
| **Extravasation** | Suspected injury needs immediate local extravasation management. For **norepinephrine or dopamine**, labels specify adult infiltration of **phentolamine 5–10 mg in 10–15 mL normal saline**, as soon as possible; local benefit described within **12 h**. This is **not a vasopressin antidote protocol**. ([Norepinephrine label](https://www.dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=728734e7-1684-4b37-980c-f1dfd408b436&type=display); [Dopamine label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2025/018826s051lbl.pdf)) |

## Local Anesthetic Maxima

### Adult single-dose limits

Content version: 2026-09-27-audit1. Section ID: `local-anesthetic-maxima-adult-single-dose-limits`.

| Agent / check | Plain · with epinephrine · essential limits |
|---|---|
| **Scope** | Adult infiltration/subcutaneous reference ceilings, **not guaranteed safe doses** and not spinal, epidural, IV regional, topical-airway or liposomal-bupivacaine protocols. Use the lower weight-based or absolute limit; reduce for patient/site risk and follow the actual product label. |
| **Lidocaine** | Plain **4.5 mg/kg, max 300 mg**; with epinephrine **7 mg/kg, max 500 mg**. ([Iowa protocols](https://iowaprotocols.medicine.uiowa.edu/protocols/maximum-recommended-doses-and-duration-local-anesthetics)) |
| **Bupivacaine** | Plain **2–2.5 mg/kg, max 175 mg**; with epinephrine **2.5–3 mg/kg, max 225 mg**. Listed total **400 mg/24 h**, but repeat dosing requires individualized assessment, not automatic reuse of the single-dose ceiling. ([Iowa protocols](https://iowaprotocols.medicine.uiowa.edu/protocols/maximum-recommended-doses-and-duration-local-anesthetics)) |
| **Ropivacaine** | Plain **2–3 mg/kg, max 225 mg** for infiltration in this reference. Do not automatically increase the ceiling because epinephrine is added; verify route/product and patient risk. ([Iowa protocols](https://iowaprotocols.medicine.uiowa.edu/protocols/maximum-recommended-doses-and-duration-local-anesthetics)) |
| **Mepivacaine** | Plain **4.5–5 mg/kg, max 400 mg**; with epinephrine **6.6 mg/kg, max 500 mg**. Levonordefrin-containing products have a different absolute limit in the reference. ([Iowa protocols](https://iowaprotocols.medicine.uiowa.edu/protocols/maximum-recommended-doses-and-duration-local-anesthetics)) |
| **Chloroprocaine** | Plain **10–12 mg/kg, max 800 mg**; with epinephrine **14 mg/kg, max 1000 mg** for infiltration. Not intrathecal dosing. ([Iowa protocols](https://iowaprotocols.medicine.uiowa.edu/protocols/maximum-recommended-doses-and-duration-local-anesthetics)) |
| **Concentration conversion** | **1% = 10 mg/mL; 0.5% = 5 mg/mL; 0.25% = 2.5 mg/mL; 2% = 20 mg/mL.** Convert total mg before calculating volume; account for every injection site and all clinicians' doses. ([Iowa protocols](https://iowaprotocols.medicine.uiowa.edu/protocols/maximum-recommended-doses-and-duration-local-anesthetics)) |
| **Toxicity risk** | Dose, vascularity/site, age, pregnancy, cardiac/hepatic/renal disease and intravascular injection matter. A dose below the listed maximum can still cause LAST; have lipid rescue immediately available. ([Iowa protocols](https://iowaprotocols.medicine.uiowa.edu/protocols/maximum-recommended-doses-and-duration-local-anesthetics); [ASRA](https://asra.com/docs/default-source/guidelines-articles/local-anesthetic-systemic-toxicity-rgb.pdf?sfvrsn=33b348e_2)) |
| **Combined local anesthetics** | **Toxicity is additive.** Account for all agents and all clinicians' doses; do not treat each drug's individual maximum as independently available when combining agents. Reduce the total plan and monitor for neurologic/cardiovascular toxicity. ([Bupivacaine label](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?id=4704)) |

### LAST rescue

Content version: 2026-09-27-audit1. Section ID: `local-anesthetic-maxima-last-rescue`.

| Intervention | ASRA quick reference |
|---|---|
| **Immediate response** | Stop local anesthetic, call for help, secure oxygenation/ventilation and obtain LAST kit. Consider early **20% lipid emulsion**; benzodiazepine preferred for seizures. ([ASRA checklist](https://asra.com/docs/default-source/guidelines-articles/local-anesthetic-systemic-toxicity-rgb.pdf?sfvrsn=33b348e_2)) |
| **Lipid: smaller patient** | ASRA weight-based branch: **1.5 mL/kg IV over 2–3 min**, then **0.25 mL/kg/min**. Checklist labels this branch <70 kg; use weight-based dosing at the boundary per local protocol. ([ASRA checklist](https://asra.com/docs/default-source/guidelines-articles/local-anesthetic-systemic-toxicity-rgb.pdf?sfvrsn=33b348e_2)) |
| **Lipid: >70 kg** | Approximately **100 mL IV over 2–3 min**, then **250 mL over 15–20 min**. ([ASRA checklist](https://asra.com/docs/default-source/guidelines-articles/local-anesthetic-systemic-toxicity-rgb.pdf?sfvrsn=33b348e_2)) |
| **Persistent instability** | Repeat bolus and double infusion. Continue lipid **>15 min after hemodynamic stability**; **maximum total 12 mL/kg**. Consider extracorporeal support. ([ASRA checklist](https://asra.com/docs/default-source/guidelines-articles/local-anesthetic-systemic-toxicity-rgb.pdf?sfvrsn=33b348e_2)) |

| **Observation after stabilization** | ASRA: observe **2 h after seizure**, **4–6 h after cardiovascular instability**, and as appropriate after cardiac arrest. Individualize monitored disposition and investigate the event. ([ASRA checklist](https://asra.com/docs/default-source/guidelines-articles/local-anesthetic-systemic-toxicity-rgb.pdf?sfvrsn=33b348e_2)) |

## Massive Transfusion

### Adult hemorrhage chart

Content version: 2026-09-27-audit1. Section ID: `massive-transfusion-adult-hemorrhage-chart`.

| Action / product | Pertinent target or dose |
|---|---|
| **Activate & control source** | Activate the institutional **MTP early**, call blood bank and obtain surgical/procedural hemorrhage control. Establish large-bore access, warming and frequent blood gas/CBC/coagulation/fibrinogen/ionized-calcium assessment; use viscoelastic testing when available. ([Trauma guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9977110/)) |
| **Balanced products** | Follow the local pack/whole-blood protocol. Trauma evidence includes **RBC:plasma:platelet-equivalent 1:1:1**, not one apheresis pack per RBC unit. **One adult apheresis platelet product is equivalent to approximately 4–6 pooled platelet units**; confirm local pack composition with blood bank. Transition to lab/viscoelastic-directed therapy as soon as feasible. ([Trauma guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9977110/)) |
| **Fibrinogen** | Major bleeding with fibrinogen **≤1.5 g/L (150 mg/dL)** or functional deficit: fibrinogen concentrate or cryoprecipitate; guideline initial fibrinogen replacement **3–4 g**. Cryo content varies; ask blood bank for the equivalent local dose. Obstetric protocols may use higher thresholds. ([Trauma guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9977110/)) |
| **Platelets** | Trauma ongoing bleeding: maintain **>50 × 10⁹/L**; traumatic brain injury **>100 × 10⁹/L**. Initial adult dose: **one apheresis pack** or local pooled equivalent, then reassess. ([Trauma guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9977110/)) |
| **Calcium** | Follow **ionized calcium**, aiming for normal range, approximately **1.1–1.3 mmol/L**. Common adult replacement: **calcium chloride 1 g IV** or **calcium gluconate 1–3 g IV**, with reassessment and local infusion/access protocol. CaCl is more tissue-injurious; these salts are **not mg-for-mg equivalent**. ([Stanford manual](https://web.stanford.edu/dept/anesthesia/em/epic-manual.pdf); [Trauma guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9977110/)) |
| **TXA: traumatic hemorrhage** | **1 g IV over 10 min**, then **1 g over 8 h**, as early as possible and **within 3 h of injury**. Do not wait for viscoelastic results. This is a trauma regimen, not a universal surgical/obstetric dose. ([Trauma guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9977110/)) |
| **Temperature / endpoints** | Warm patient and products; target normothermia. Trend perfusion, lactate/base deficit, coagulation and calcium. Hemoglobin during active exsanguination is not a stand-alone trigger; do not apply permissive hypotension indiscriminately, especially with TBI. ([Trauma guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9977110/)) |

## Neuromuscular Blockade

### Intubation & contraindications

Content version: 2026-09-27-audit1. Section ID: `neuromuscular-blockade-intubation-contraindications`.

| Medication / check | Adult IV dose · critical details |
|---|---|
| **Scope** | Paralysis is not sedation or analgesia. Ensure adequate anesthesia, ability to ventilate and quantitative neuromuscular monitoring; redose to measured block rather than elapsed time alone. |
| **Succinylcholine** | Adult RSI **1.5 mg/kg IV** in Merck; routine label average **0.6 mg/kg** is a different intubation context. Rapid onset/short usual duration, but prolonged paralysis is possible. Do not present the routine-label dose as equivalent to an RSI dose. ([Merck](https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/critical-care-medicine/respiratory-arrest/medications-to-aid-intubation); [Label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2022/215143s003lbl.pdf)) |
| **Succinylcholine: avoid** | **MH susceptibility, skeletal myopathy, hyperkalemia risk**, or after the acute phase of major burns, extensive denervation, multiple trauma or upper motor-neuron injury. Timing of risk onset/duration is not reliably fixed; normal baseline K does not remove receptor-upregulation risk. Known low plasma cholinesterase: prolonged block, not recommended. ([Label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2022/215143s003lbl.pdf)) |
| **Rocuronium** | **0.6 mg/kg IV** routine intubation; **1.2 mg/kg** rapid intubation regimen. Higher doses prolong block. Reversible with sugammadex when criteria are met. ([Rocuronium label](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?format=PDF&id=4593); [Sugammadex label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=d9d4467c-97ae-462a-9722-cc8d62db3e83)) |
| **Vecuronium** | **0.08–0.1 mg/kg IV** for intubation. Monitor duration carefully with organ dysfunction; sugammadex can reverse routine block but immediate 16 mg/kg rescue after vecuronium has not been studied. ([Vecuronium label](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?id=4648); [Sugammadex label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=d9d4467c-97ae-462a-9722-cc8d62db3e83)) |
| **Cisatracurium** | **0.15–0.2 mg/kg IV** for intubation; **not recommended for RSI** because of onset time. **Sugammadex does not reverse it**; allow recovery and use appropriate anticholinesterase reversal when indicated. ([Cisatracurium label](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?id=15414); [ASA guideline](https://pubs.asahq.org/anesthesiology/article/138/1/13/137379/2023-American-Society-of-Anesthesiologists)) |

### Reversal & recovery

Content version: 2026-09-27. Section ID: `neuromuscular-blockade-reversal-recovery`.

| Intervention | Adult dose · monitoring |
|---|---|
| **Sugammadex: T2 present** | **2 mg/kg IV** for rocuronium/vecuronium block once the **second TOF twitch** reappears. Dose by **actual body weight**, bolus over approximately **10 sec**. ([Label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=d9d4467c-97ae-462a-9722-cc8d62db3e83)) |
| **Sugammadex: deep block** | **4 mg/kg IV** when **1–2 post-tetanic counts** and no TOF twitches. Continue airway/ventilation support until recovery is confirmed. ([Label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=d9d4467c-97ae-462a-9722-cc8d62db3e83)) |
| **Sugammadex: immediate reversal** | **16 mg/kg IV** approximately **3 min after rocuronium 1.2 mg/kg**, when clinically necessary. Not a proven immediate-reversal regimen for vecuronium or pediatric patients; reversal does not replace an emergency airway plan. ([Label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=d9d4467c-97ae-462a-9722-cc8d62db3e83)) |
| **Sugammadex cautions** | Not recommended with **CrCl <30 mL/min**; anaphylaxis and severe bradycardia can occur. Hormonal contraception requires **7 days of additional nonhormonal protection**. Does not reverse succinylcholine/cisatracurium. ([Label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=d9d4467c-97ae-462a-9722-cc8d62db3e83)) |
| **Neostigmine: minimal block** | At quantitative TOF ratio **0.4 to <0.9**, ASA supports approximately **30 mcg/kg IV**, **do not exceed 40 mcg/kg** at this depth; **15–30 mcg/kg** may suffice when ratio >0.6. Give an appropriate antimuscarinic concurrently per product/local protocol. Not reliable rescue for deep block. ([ASA guideline](https://pubs.asahq.org/anesthesiology/article/138/1/13/137379/2023-American-Society-of-Anesthesiologists); [Anesthesia reference](https://anesthesia.bidmc.harvard.edu/ADEL/Documents/med_list.pdf)) |
| **Extubation criterion** | Quantitative monitoring at the **adductor pollicis**, confirm **TOF ratio ≥0.9 before extubation**. Four visible twitches or head lift alone do not prove recovery. Spontaneous quantitative ratio ≥0.9 does not need pharmacologic reversal. ([ASA guideline](https://pubs.asahq.org/anesthesiology/article/138/1/13/137379/2023-American-Society-of-Anesthesiologists)) |

## Normal Hemodynamics

### Adult measurements

Content version: 2026-09-27. Section ID: `normal-hemodynamics-adult-measurements`.

| Measurement | Typical resting range · units |
|---|---|
| **Scope** | Reference ranges vary with source, measurement conditions and patient physiology. These are not universal resuscitation targets. |
| **CVP** | **2–6 mmHg**. Interpret with waveform, ventilation and clinical context; not a stand-alone volume-responsiveness test. ([PAC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7917533/)) |
| **Pulmonary artery pressure** | Systolic **15–30 mmHg**; diastolic **8–15 mmHg**. ([PAC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7917533/)) |
| **PAWP / PCWP** | Pulmonary artery wedge pressure **6–12 mmHg**. Accurate position/waveform and appropriate interpretation are essential. ([PAC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7917533/)) |
| **Cardiac output** | **4–8 L/min**. ([PAC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7917533/)) |
| **Cardiac index** | **2.5–4 L/min/m²**; CO indexed to body surface area. ([PAC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7917533/)) |
| **Stroke volume** | **60–100 mL/beat**. SV = CO × 1000 ÷ HR, when CO is in L/min. ([Hemodynamic table](https://accessanesthesiology.mhmedical.com/content.aspx?bookid=1944&sectionid=143523327)) |
| **SVR** | **800–1200 dyn·s·cm⁻⁵**. SVR = 80 × (MAP − RAP) ÷ CO. ([PAC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7917533/)) |
| **PVR** | **<250 dyn·s·cm⁻⁵** in this reference table. PVR = 80 × (mean PAP − PAWP) ÷ CO. A reference range is not the diagnostic threshold for pulmonary hypertension. ([PAC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7917533/)) |

## Pre-Op Clearance Guidelines

### When a separate clearance visit may not be needed

Content version: 2026-09-27. Section ID: `preop-clearance-visit`.

**No separate clearance visit does not mean no preoperative assessment.** ASA standards call for evaluation before anesthesia care, with modification and documentation under exceptional circumstances; a separate PCP or specialist consultation is a different service, selected according to the clinical situation ([ASA Basic Standards for Preanesthesia Care](https://www.asahq.org/~/media/Sites/ASAHQ/Files/Public/Resources/standards-guidelines/basic-standards-for-preanesthesia-care.pdf); [Merck Manual: Preoperative Evaluation](https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/special-subjects/care-of-the-surgical-patient/preoperative-evaluation)).

#### Situations that may not require an additional clearance appointment

- **Emergency surgery:** Do not delay life- or limb-saving treatment to obtain an outpatient clearance appointment; perform the rapid assessment feasible, especially reviewing allergies, anticoagulants, bleeding history, and previous anesthetic complications ([Merck Manual](https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/special-subjects/care-of-the-surgical-patient/preoperative-evaluation)).
- **Healthy, asymptomatic patient having a low-risk procedure:** A separate medical consultation may be unnecessary when the existing surgical/anesthesia assessment identifies no issue requiring additional evaluation or optimization; treat this as an individualized decision, not a blanket exemption from assessment ([Merck Manual](https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/special-subjects/care-of-the-surgical-patient/preoperative-evaluation); [ASA preanesthesia standards](https://www.asahq.org/~/media/Sites/ASAHQ/Files/Public/Resources/standards-guidelines/basic-standards-for-preanesthesia-care.pdf)).
- **Minor superficial procedures:** Superficial surgery falls within low-risk procedural categories, supporting limited, indication-based cardiovascular testing rather than an automatic cardiac workup; low procedural risk alone does not establish a universal exemption from a separate visit ([VCMC institutional guideline](https://vcportal.ventura.org/VCHCA/Hospitals/Medical%20Staff%20Services/CPG/vcmc-pre-operative-cpg-4-2015.pdf); [2024 AHA/ACC guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001285)).
- **Routine cataract surgery:** SAMBA recommends no testing unless indicated independently of the procedure; this supports avoiding unnecessary testing, not declaring that all preoperative assessment or consultation is prohibited ([SAMBA cataract position statement](https://samba.memberclicks.net/assets/docs/SAMBA_Statements/Preoperative_Care_for_Cataract_Surgery__The.11.pdf)).
- **Low cardiovascular risk:** Routine stress testing is not recommended for low-risk patients or low-risk procedures, and routine ECG is not recommended for asymptomatic patients undergoing low-risk surgery; these are testing recommendations, not rules eliminating all evaluation ([AHA recommendations, slides 29–34](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).

#### Findings that are not stand-alone exemptions

- **Age below 40 or 50:** Do not use a universal age cutoff to waive evaluation; the cited VCMC document uses age thresholds for selected tests, not an age-based exemption from preoperative assessment ([VCMC institutional guideline](https://vcportal.ventura.org/VCHCA/Hospitals/Medical%20Staff%20Services/CPG/vcmc-pre-operative-cpg-4-2015.pdf)).
- **No chronic disease or daily prescriptions:** These may support a lower-risk profile, but absence of diagnoses or medications is not a substitute for reviewing symptoms, medical/anesthetic history, and relevant examination findings ([ASA preanesthesia standards](https://www.asahq.org/~/media/Sites/ASAHQ/Files/Public/Resources/standards-guidelines/basic-standards-for-preanesthesia-care.pdf)).
- **Local anesthesia alone:** Do not present local anesthesia as an automatic exemption; assess the patient and procedure rather than assuming that the anesthetic label eliminates risk or consultation needs ([Merck Manual](https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/special-subjects/care-of-the-surgical-patient/preoperative-evaluation)).
- **Stable chronic disease:** Its presence does not automatically require a specialist “clearance”; seek consultation when it would help evaluate a relevant problem, optimize treatment, or change perioperative management ([Merck Manual](https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/special-subjects/care-of-the-surgical-patient/preoperative-evaluation)).

#### Clinical takeaway

“A separate PCP or specialist clearance visit may not be necessary when the surgical/anesthesia evaluation is sufficient and no additional clinical issue needs assessment or optimization. Confirm applicable facility requirements; do not confuse no additional consultation with no preoperative assessment.”

Scope note: This takeaway is a synthesis, not a quoted AHA exemption rule; the older VCMC document is an institutional example, not a current national policy. Its local testing requirements and the commercial websites’ broad exemption language are not adopted as universal Luma criteria ([VCMC institutional guideline](https://vcportal.ventura.org/VCHCA/Hospitals/Medical%20Staff%20Services/CPG/vcmc-pre-operative-cpg-4-2015.pdf); [ASA preanesthesia standards](https://www.asahq.org/~/media/Sites/ASAHQ/Files/Public/Resources/standards-guidelines/basic-standards-for-preanesthesia-care.pdf)).

### At a glance: proceed, evaluate, or defer

Content version: 2026-09-27. Section ID: `preop-proceed-defer`.

- **Emergency surgery:** Perform the focused assessment and management feasible without delaying life- or limb-saving intervention; routine elective testing pathways do not take priority over emergency care ([AHA/ACC guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001285)).
- **Active cardiac condition:** Postpone elective surgery for evaluation and treatment of acute coronary syndrome, decompensated heart failure, or unstable arrhythmia ([guideline algorithm overview](https://www.ccjm.org/content/92/4/213)).
- **Low risk or adequate functional capacity:** Routine ischemia testing is not recommended when predicted perioperative risk is low, the procedure is low risk, or functional capacity is adequate with stable symptoms ([AHA recommendations, slides 32–34](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Elevated risk plus poor or unknown functional capacity:** Consider selective further evaluation only when findings could change management; this combination does not automatically require a stress test or cancellation ([AHA/ACC guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001285)).
- **Important risk modifier:** Address severe valve disease, pulmonary hypertension, congenital heart disease, prior PCI/CABG, recent stroke, a cardiac device, or frailty even when the calculated risk appears reassuring ([guideline algorithm overview](https://www.ccjm.org/content/92/4/213)).

### Surgical urgency

Content version: 2026-09-27. Section ID: `preop-urgency`.

- **Emergency:** Immediate threat to life or limb; generally less than 2 hours available ([AHA/ACC guideline, section 1.5](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001285)).
- **Urgent:** Threat to life or limb; generally 2 to less than 24 hours available ([AHA/ACC guideline, section 1.5](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001285)).
- **Time-sensitive:** May be delayed up to 3 months for evaluation and management without adversely affecting outcomes ([AHA/ACC guideline, section 1.5](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001285)).
- **Elective:** Can be delayed to complete appropriate evaluation and treatment ([AHA/ACC guideline, section 1.5](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001285)).

### Symptoms, risk & functional capacity

Content version: 2026-09-27. Section ID: `preop-risk`.

- **Symptoms requiring attention:** Chest pain, dyspnea, unexplained palpitations, tachycardia, syncope, or a murmur should inform targeted cardiovascular evaluation ([AHA recommendations, slide 29](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Risk estimate:** Combine patient and procedural factors using a validated tool, such as RCRI, MICA, or ACS NSQIP; the guideline does not require a single preferred calculator ([AHA/ACC guideline, section 3.1](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001285)).
- **Risk categories:** Low risk is predicted major adverse cardiovascular event risk below 1%; elevated risk is 1% or greater, interpreted using the chosen tool and its outcome definitions ([AHA/ACC guideline, section 1.5](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001285)).
- **Functional capacity:** Use structured assessment, such as the Duke Activity Status Index (DASI), particularly before elevated-risk surgery; less than 4 METs or DASI 34 or lower identifies poor capacity for the guideline’s testing pathway ([AHA recommendations, slides 22–23 and 32](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Frailty:** A validated frailty assessment can help guide care before elevated-risk surgery in older adults and younger patients with suspected frailty ([AHA recommendations, slide 24](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).

### ECG, echo, biomarkers & stress testing

Content version: 2026-09-27. Section ID: `preop-testing`.

#### ECG

- **Reasonable before elevated-risk surgery:** Known coronary disease, significant arrhythmia, peripheral arterial disease, cerebrovascular disease, significant structural heart disease, or cardiovascular symptoms ([AHA recommendations, slide 29](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **New ECG abnormality:** Further evaluation is reasonable for findings such as new ST/T changes, pathologic Q waves, Mobitz II or higher AV block, bundle branch block, QT prolongation, or atrial fibrillation ([AHA recommendations, slide 30](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Asymptomatic patient without known CVD:** A baseline ECG may be considered for elevated-risk surgery, but routine ECG is not recommended for asymptomatic patients having low-risk procedures ([AHA recommendations, slide 30](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).

#### Echocardiography / ventricular function

- **Obtain LV-function assessment:** New dyspnea, examination findings of heart failure, or suspected new or worsening ventricular dysfunction ([AHA recommendations, slide 31](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Reassess known heart failure when clinically changed:** Worsening dyspnea or another change in status supports repeat assessment; routine repeat LV-function testing is not recommended in asymptomatic, clinically stable patients ([AHA recommendations, slide 31](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Suspected valve disease:** Obtain preoperative echocardiography for suspected moderate/severe aortic stenosis before elevated-risk elective surgery and for suspected moderate/severe valvular regurgitation before elective surgery ([AHA recommendations, slides 56 and 59](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).

#### Biomarkers

- **Who qualifies for consideration:** Elevated-risk surgery plus known CVD, age 65 or older, or age 45 or older with symptoms suggestive of CVD; BNP/NT-proBNP measurement is reasonable, and troponin measurement may be reasonable ([AHA recommendations, slide 27](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Algorithm thresholds:** BNP greater than 92 ng/L, NT-proBNP 300 ng/L or greater, or troponin above the assay’s 99th-percentile upper reference limit are abnormal thresholds used in the pathway, not stand-alone cancellation criteria ([AHA algorithm, slide 37](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Elevated result:** Discuss whether to proceed with an adapted plan, investigate further, or consider alternatives; biomarker elevation alone does not mandate stress testing or coronary intervention ([guideline algorithm overview](https://www.ccjm.org/content/92/4/213)).

#### Stress testing, CCTA, and coronary intervention

- **Selective stress testing:** May be considered when elevated-risk surgery, elevated calculated cardiovascular risk, and poor/unknown functional capacity coexist, provided the result could change management ([AHA/ACC guideline, section 4.3](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001285)).
- **Selective coronary CT angiography:** May be considered in the same selected population to identify high-risk coronary anatomy; neither CCTA nor stress testing is routine for low-risk patients or those with adequate capacity and stable symptoms ([AHA recommendations, slides 32–34](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Avoid routine invasive angiography:** It is not recommended solely to improve perioperative outcomes ([AHA recommendations, slide 35](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Revascularization:** Treat acute coronary syndrome as appropriate and defer elective surgery; for hemodynamically significant left-main stenosis of at least 50%, revascularization and deferral are reasonable, whereas routine prophylactic revascularization for non-left-main disease is not recommended ([AHA recommendations, slide 39](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).

### Timing after PCI, stents, stroke or TIA

Content version: 2026-09-27. Section ID: `preop-pci-stroke`.

- **Balloon angioplasty without a stent:** Delay elective surgery at least 14 days ([AHA recommendations, slide 73](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Drug-eluting stent for acute coronary syndrome:** If elective surgery requires interruption of one or more antiplatelet agents, ideally delay at least 12 months ([AHA recommendations, slide 73](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Drug-eluting stent for chronic coronary disease:** If antiplatelet interruption is required, delaying elective surgery at least 6 months is reasonable ([AHA recommendations, slide 73](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Time-sensitive surgery after a drug-eluting stent:** May be considered at 3 months or later when antiplatelet interruption is necessary and the harm of delaying surgery exceeds the cardiovascular risk ([AHA recommendations, slide 74](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Recent stent danger window:** Elective surgery requiring antiplatelet interruption within 30 days of a bare-metal or drug-eluting stent is potentially harmful ([AHA recommendations, slide 74](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Stroke or TIA:** Delaying elective surgery at least 3 months after the most recent event is reasonable ([AHA recommendations, slide 66](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).

### Valve disease & high-risk cardiac conditions

Content version: 2026-09-27. Section ID: `preop-cardiac-conditions`.

- **Severe aortic stenosis:** Evaluate the need for valve intervention before elective surgery; asymptomatic moderate/severe AS with normal LV systolic function documented by echo within the past year can reasonably proceed to low-risk elective surgery ([AHA recommendations, slide 56](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Severe mitral stenosis:** Evaluate the need for valve intervention before elective surgery; when intervention is not possible beforehand, invasive monitoring is reasonable and perioperative heart-rate control may be considered ([AHA recommendations, slide 58](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Moderate/severe mitral regurgitation:** Elective surgery is reasonable when asymptomatic, LV systolic function is normal, and estimated pulmonary artery systolic pressure is below 50 mm Hg ([AHA recommendations, slide 59](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Moderate/severe aortic regurgitation:** Elective surgery is reasonable when asymptomatic with normal LV systolic function, specified as LVEF above 55%; patients meeting valve-intervention indications warrant consideration of intervention before elevated-risk elective surgery ([AHA recommendations, slide 59](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Compensated heart failure:** Continue guideline-directed therapy unless contraindicated, except for the preoperative SGLT2-inhibitor hold; new decompensation requires reassessment rather than routine progression through the elective pathway ([AHA recommendations, slide 42](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en); [algorithm overview](https://www.ccjm.org/content/92/4/213)).
- **Severe pulmonary hypertension:** Before elevated-risk surgery, specialized PH consultation and planning for monitoring/postoperative care are reasonable; continue established pulmonary arterial hypertension therapies ([AHA recommendations, slides 46–47](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Congenital heart disease or LVAD:** Intermediate/elevated-risk congenital lesions require adult congenital-heart specialist consultation before elective surgery; LVAD patients require coordination with their LVAD team ([AHA recommendations, slides 48 and 55](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy:** Continue established beta-blocker or nondihydropyridine calcium-channel-blocker therapy and plan to avoid tachycardia, reduced preload/afterload, and factors that worsen dynamic outflow obstruction ([AHA recommendations, slides 44–45](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Pacemaker/ICD:** If electromagnetic interference is anticipated, establish a device-specific plan before surgery, including device type, manufacturer/model, pacing dependence, known magnet response, and restoration of any altered functions ([AHA recommendations, slides 62–64](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).

### Blood pressure & perioperative medications

Content version: 2026-09-27. Section ID: `preop-medications`.

- **Poorly controlled hypertension:** Deferral may be considered for elective elevated-risk surgery when cardiovascular risk factors coexist with a recent history of SBP at least 180 or DBP at least 110 mm Hg before the day of surgery; this is not a universal automatic cancellation threshold ([AHA recommendations, slide 40](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Beta blockers:** Continue stable therapy as clinically appropriate; if newly indicated, initiate sufficiently early, optimally more than 7 days before surgery, to assess tolerance and titrate, rather than starting on the day of surgery without an immediate indication ([AHA recommendations, slide 87](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Statins:** Continue existing therapy; initiate when independently indicated by ASCVD history or risk assessment, with intention for long-term use ([AHA recommendations, slide 69](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **ACE inhibitors/ARBs and other RAAS inhibitors:** For selected patients taking them for controlled hypertension before elevated-risk surgery, omission 24 hours beforehand may reduce hypotension; continuation is reasonable when prescribed for HFrEF ([AHA recommendations, slide 70](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **SGLT2 inhibitors:** Stop canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, and empagliflozin at least 3 days, and ertugliflozin at least 4 days, before scheduled surgery to reduce perioperative metabolic-acidosis risk ([AHA recommendations, slides 42 and 88](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Clonidine:** Do not newly initiate low-dose clonidine solely to reduce perioperative cardiovascular risk ([AHA recommendations, slide 71](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).

### GLP-1 / GIP–GLP-1: aspiration risk & fasting

Content version: 2026-09-27. Section ID: `preop-glp1`.

This is a separately sourced anesthesia addendum, not a recommendation attributed solely to the cardiovascular guideline. The ASA-led 2024 multisociety guidance and the 2025 SPAQI consensus differ in their dietary recommendations, so their pathways are labeled rather than combined ([multisociety guidance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11666732/); [SPAQI consensus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12597468/)).

#### Identify the medication and screen for risk

- **Medication reconciliation:** Identify the agent, formulation, daily/weekly schedule, dose, last administration, indication, recent initiation or dose increases, and gastrointestinal symptoms; include semaglutide, liraglutide, dulaglutide, and dual GIP/GLP-1 agent tirzepatide, which is included in SPAQI’s medication review ([multisociety guidance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11666732/); [SPAQI consensus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12597468/)).
- **Risk factors:** Dose escalation, higher doses, weekly formulations, nausea/vomiting, abdominal pain, dyspepsia, constipation, and other conditions causing delayed emptying or dysmotility warrant attention under the multisociety framework ([multisociety guidance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11666732/)).
- **Do not rely on symptoms alone:** Absence of gastrointestinal symptoms does not guarantee normal gastric emptying or an empty stomach ([SPAQI consensus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12597468/)).

#### Continuation and risk mitigation: ASA-led multisociety approach

- **Lower-risk patients:** Therapy may continue before elective procedures when elevated risk of delayed gastric emptying and aspiration is not identified; routine discontinuation for every patient is not the default ([multisociety guidance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11666732/)).
- **Higher-risk patients:** Consider a liquid-only diet for at least 24 hours before the procedure, an aspiration-focused anesthesia plan, and day-of-procedure gastric ultrasound when available and performed by appropriately trained clinicians ([multisociety guidance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11666732/)).
- **Suspected or confirmed retained contents:** Discuss postponement versus proceeding with an appropriately modified anesthetic, including consideration of rapid-sequence induction with tracheal intubation when general anesthesia is appropriate; taking a GLP-1 drug is not itself an automatic cancellation rule ([multisociety guidance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11666732/)).
- **If a medication hold is selected:** The optimal interruption duration is unknown; the multisociety document suggests the earlier ASA intervals of holding daily agents on the procedure day and weekly agents for 1 week when the team determines a hold is necessary, not as a universal instruction or guarantee of an empty stomach ([multisociety guidance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11666732/)).
- **Diabetes considerations:** Balance interruption against hyperglycemia and loss of treatment benefit, and coordinate any alternative treatment with the prescribing team rather than automatically substituting insulin ([multisociety guidance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11666732/)).

#### Separate guidance note: SPAQI 2025 fasting approach

- **Medication continuation:** Continue perioperatively in patients without significant gastrointestinal symptoms; SPAQI defines significant symptoms as severe nausea, vomiting, or inability to tolerate oral intake, not fullness or early satiety alone ([SPAQI consensus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12597468/)).
- **Solid food:** For these patients, fast from solids for 24 hours, allowing only clear liquids during that period until the applicable liquid cutoff ([SPAQI consensus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12597468/)).
- **High-carbohydrate clear liquids:** Fast for 8 hours when glucose content is 10% or greater ([SPAQI consensus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12597468/)).
- **No-/low-carbohydrate clear liquids:** Fast for 4 hours when glucose content is below 10%, including water ([SPAQI consensus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12597468/)).
- **Significant symptoms:** Refer for prescriber-directed diet and medication modification before elective anesthesia; severely symptomatic patients should not proceed with elective procedures ([SPAQI consensus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12597468/)).
- **Glucose safety during dietary restriction:** Patients with diabetes need an individualized glucose-monitoring and medication plan during the clear-liquid period ([SPAQI consensus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12597468/)).

#### Urgent/emergency procedures

- **Urgent/emergency surgery:** The ASA’s 2023 emergency recommendation is to proceed with full-stomach precautions rather than wait for a medication hold interval; distinguish this emergency precaution from the older blanket elective withholding approach ([ASA 2023 statement, reproduced PDF](https://www.swa10.com/uploads/6/1/4/3/61438899/glp-1.asa.2023.pdf)).
- **Diet is not the same as fasting:** “Liquid-only for 24 hours” does not mean liquids may continue until anesthesia; the 2024 multisociety document does not specify the same detailed liquid cutoffs as SPAQI, so confirm the institution’s adopted diet and fasting instructions ([multisociety guidance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11666732/); [SPAQI consensus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12597468/)).

Safety reminder: **Do not automatically stop every GLP-1 medication or assume that a 7-day hold eliminates aspiration risk. Confirm the patient-specific plan and the facility’s adopted fasting protocol.**

### Antiplatelets & anticoagulant planning

Content version: 2026-09-27. Section ID: `preop-antithrombotics`.

- **Prior PCI:** Continue aspirin 75–100 mg if possible, with multidisciplinary assessment of bleeding and thrombosis risk ([AHA recommendations, slides 72–74](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Early surgery after stenting:** For time-sensitive surgery within 30 days of a bare-metal stent or less than 3 months after a drug-eluting stent, continue dual antiplatelet therapy unless bleeding risk outweighs protection against stent thrombosis ([AHA recommendations, slide 74](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **If interruption has been selected:** The guideline lists platelet-function recovery intervals of aspirin 4 days, clopidogrel 5–7 days, prasugrel 7–10 days, and ticagrelor 3–5 days; these are not instructions to stop every patient’s therapy ([AHA recommendations, slide 76](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Oral anticoagulants:** Base interruption on the agent, renal clearance, bleeding risk, and thrombotic risk; routine heparin bridging is not recommended for most patients, while selected high-thrombotic-risk patients interrupting a vitamin K antagonist may benefit ([AHA recommendations, slides 78–84](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Neuraxial planning:** Do not treat general surgical interruption intervals as stand-alone authorization for neuraxial procedures; the guideline specifically calls for additional team-based consideration where bleeding consequences are high, including neuraxial anesthesia ([AHA recommendations, slide 82](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).

### Diabetes, OSA & anemia

Content version: 2026-09-27-audit1. Section ID: `preop-other-readiness`.

- **Diabetes:** HbA1c testing is reasonable in patients with diabetes or at risk for diabetes if no result is available within the preceding 3 months ([AHA recommendations, slide 88](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Metformin: guidelines differ.** The 2024 AHA/ACC cardiovascular guideline considers perioperative continuation reasonable, whereas ADA 2026 recommends holding metformin and other oral glucose-lowering agents on the day of surgery/procedure. Follow the institution's adopted diabetes medication plan and assess renal function/acute illness; do not interpret the cardiovascular recommendation as a universal instruction to continue. See **Pre-Op Glucose** for the ADA-based chart ([AHA recommendations, slide 88](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en); [ADA 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12690180/)).
- **OSA:** Screening with a validated questionnaire is reasonable to assess perioperative complication risk ([AHA recommendations, slide 67](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Iron-deficiency anemia:** Preoperative oral or IV iron is reasonable before elective surgery to increase hemoglobin and reduce transfusion requirements ([AHA recommendations, slide 96](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).

### Document the perioperative plan

Content version: 2026-09-27-audit1. Section ID: `preop-documentation`.

Documentation prompts, not a validated clearance score:

- **Assessment:** Procedure/urgency, symptoms, calculated risk, functional capacity, and important risk modifiers.
- **Outstanding issues:** Additional testing or consultation, who will review results, and what findings would change the plan.
- **Medication/device plan:** Specific instructions, antithrombotic coordination, and any pacemaker/ICD arrangements.
- **Disposition:** Proceed with the agreed plan, obtain further evaluation, or defer elective surgery for optimization.
- **Communication:** Document the team’s risk-benefit discussion and the patient’s preferences.

These documentation prompts synthesize the assessment and team-planning principles in the [AHA/ACC perioperative guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001285) and [ASA preanesthesia standards](https://www.asahq.org/~/media/Sites/ASAHQ/Files/Public/Resources/standards-guidelines/basic-standards-for-preanesthesia-care.pdf); they are not a quoted or validated clearance checklist.

## Pre-Op Glucose

### Hyperglycemia & medication readiness

Content version: 2026-09-27-audit1. Section ID: `preop-glucose-hyperglycemia-medication-readiness`.

| Check | Adult perioperative action |
|---|---|
| **Scope** | ADA and SAMBA recommendations address different settings. Do not label a glucose cutoff or insulin scale as an “ASA clearance rule.” Individualize to urgency, comorbidity and local protocol. |
| **Confirm & assess** | Confirm unexpected glucose with point-of-care/laboratory testing; assess hydration, symptoms, medications and possible ketosis/acidosis. **CGM alone is insufficient intraoperatively.** SGLT2-associated DKA can occur without marked hyperglycemia. ([ADA 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12690180/)) |
| **ADA target** | Generally **100–180 mg/dL before, during and after surgery**, individualized for hypoglycemia risk and procedure. Check at least **every 2–4 h while NPO**; more frequent checks may be needed with IV insulin or instability. ([ADA 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12690180/)) |
| **SAMBA ambulatory context** | Intraoperative targets may be **180–250 mg/dL**, depending on patient/procedure. **Hyperglycemia alone does not require cancellation** in the absence of DKA/HHS; use clinical assessment, not an automatic universal threshold. This is not the general ADA target. ([SAMBA 2024 summary](https://www.apsf.org/in-the-literature/society-for-ambulatory-anesthesia-updated-consensus-statement-on-perioperative-blood-glucose-management/)) |
| **Treat elevated glucose** | Use the institution's **patient-specific correction-insulin protocol**, accounting for usual insulin, sensitivity and renal function; SAMBA supports **SC insulin** for ambulatory hyperglycemia. Avoid repeated untracked doses/stacking. A single universal units-per-glucose-band table is not appropriate. ([ADA 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12690180/); [SAMBA](https://www.apsf.org/in-the-literature/society-for-ambulatory-anesthesia-updated-consensus-statement-on-perioperative-blood-glucose-management/)) |
| **DKA / HHS concern** | Metabolic decompensation needs urgent evaluation/treatment, not routine clearance or a one-time correction dose. Postpone elective procedures while treating; coordinate emergency surgery with resuscitation and diabetes/critical-care teams. ([SAMBA](https://www.apsf.org/in-the-literature/society-for-ambulatory-anesthesia-updated-consensus-statement-on-perioperative-blood-glucose-management/); [ADA 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12690180/)) |
| **A1C** | Elective optimization goal **<8% within 3 months**, individualized. **Do not postpone based on A1C alone.** ([ADA 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12690180/)) |
| **Basal insulin** | Typical long-acting analog dose **75–80%** of usual; NPH **50%**, coordinated with dosing time/local plan. Type 1 diabetes requires basal insulin even while fasting; adjust individually rather than omit. ([ADA 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12690180/)) |
| **Oral agents / SGLT2** | **ADA 2026:** hold metformin/other oral glucose-lowering agents on procedure day. The AHA/ACC cardiovascular guideline permits metformin continuation; **follow the adopted institutional diabetes plan**, not conflicting defaults. Stop SGLT2 inhibitors **3 days before elective surgery; ertugliflozin 4 days**. Nonelective surgery: monitor for euglycemic DKA. Restart only after clinical/nutritional recovery and risk reassessment. ([ADA 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12690180/); [AHA/ACC 2024](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)) |
| **GLP-1 / dual GIP–GLP-1** | **Individualized, not a blanket hold.** Consider escalation phase, dose, GI symptoms and aspiration risk; low-risk patients may continue. Higher-risk plans can include **24-h liquid nutrition**, gastric ultrasound or full-stomach precautions. See Pre-op Clearance Guidelines for the detailed pathway. ([ADA 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12690180/)) |

## SVT, AFib & Cardioversion

### SVT & AF with RVR

Content version: 2026-09-27-audit1. Section ID: `svt-afib-cardioversion-svt-af-with-rvr`.

| Situation / drug | Adult treatment · critical limits |
|---|---|
| **Unstable tachyarrhythmia** | Hypotension, shock, altered mentation, ischemic discomfort or acute HF **attributable to the rhythm**: immediate synchronized cardioversion. Sedate if feasible without delaying treatment; treat reversible causes. Sinus tachycardia needs treatment of its cause. ([2025 AHA](https://www.heart.org/-/media/CPR-Files/CPR-Guidelines-Files/2025-Algorithms/Algorithm-ACLS-Tachycardia-250514.pdf)) |
| **Stable regular narrow SVT** | Vagal maneuvers, then **adenosine 6 mg rapid IV push + flush**, followed by **12 mg** if needed. In a wide-complex rhythm, adenosine is considered **only if regular and monomorphic**; never for irregular pre-excited AF. ([2025 AHA](https://www.heart.org/-/media/CPR-Files/CPR-Guidelines-Files/2025-Algorithms/Algorithm-ACLS-Tachycardia-250514.pdf); [AF guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001193)) |
| **Metoprolol tartrate** | Stable AF rate control: **2.5–5 mg IV over 2 min**, up to **3 doses; max 15 mg**. Avoid shock/decompensated HF, severe bradycardia/block; reassess BP. **Not for pre-excited AF.** ([AF guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001193); [Label](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?id=17205)) |
| **Diltiazem** | **0.25 mg/kg actual weight IV over 2 min**; if inadequate response, repeat after **15 min** with **0.35 mg/kg over 2 min**. Infusion **5–15 mg/h**; label usual start **10 mg/h**, some respond to 5 mg/h. For stable AF with **EF >40%**; avoid moderate/severe LV systolic dysfunction, hypotension and pre-excited AF. Avoid concomitant IV beta-blockers or administration within a few hours per label. ([AF guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001193); [Diltiazem label](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?id=4408)) |
| **Digoxin** | Selected patients when beta-blockers/CCBs are ineffective or contraindicated: **0.25–0.5 mg IV over several minutes**, then **0.25 mg every 6 h**, guideline maximum **1.5 mg/24 h**. Individualize a lower total for renal impairment, older age or low lean mass; slower onset. **Not for pre-excited AF; do not extrapolate this routine AF regimen to accessory-pathway tachycardia.** See WPW Syndrome. ([AF guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001193)) |
| **Amiodarone: AF rate control** | Selected critically ill/decompensated-HF patients when other agents cannot be used: **150–300 mg IV over 1 h**, then **10–50 mg/h over 24 h**, per AF guideline. Can cardiovert: assess stroke/anticoagulation risk. **Do not use in pre-excited AF.** This is distinct from the VT/arrest regimen. ([AF guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001193)) |

### Cardioversion energies & precautions

| Rhythm / check | Adult electrical treatment |
|---|---|
| **Atrial fibrillation** | **200 J synchronized** initial energy in the 2025 AHA cardioversion algorithm; follow device-specific instructions and escalate if unsuccessful. ([2025 AHA](https://cpr.heart.org/-/media/CPR-Files/CPR-Guidelines-Files/2025-Algorithms/Algorithm-ACLS-Electrical-Cardioversion-250514.pdf?sc_lang=en)) |
| **Atrial flutter** | **200 J synchronized** in the 2025 algorithm, not the older 50–100 J starting recommendation. ([2025 AHA](https://cpr.heart.org/-/media/CPR-Files/CPR-Guidelines-Files/2025-Algorithms/Algorithm-ACLS-Electrical-Cardioversion-250514.pdf?sc_lang=en)) |
| **Regular narrow tachycardia** | **100 J synchronized**. ([2025 AHA](https://cpr.heart.org/-/media/CPR-Files/CPR-Guidelines-Files/2025-Algorithms/Algorithm-ACLS-Electrical-Cardioversion-250514.pdf?sc_lang=en)) |
| **Monomorphic VT with pulse** | **100 J synchronized** if cardioversion is indicated. ([2025 AHA](https://cpr.heart.org/-/media/CPR-Files/CPR-Guidelines-Files/2025-Algorithms/Algorithm-ACLS-Electrical-Cardioversion-250514.pdf?sc_lang=en)) |

([2025 AHA](https://cpr.heart.org/-/media/CPR-Files/CPR-Guidelines-Files/2025-Algorithms/Algorithm-ACLS-Electrical-Cardioversion-250514.pdf?sc_lang=en)) |
| **AF anticoagulation** | Elective AF **≥48 h or uncertain duration**: **≥3 weeks therapeutic anticoagulation or imaging to exclude thrombus** before cardioversion. Establish therapeutic anticoagulation before and continue **≥4 weeks afterward** per guideline; longer therapy depends on stroke risk. Do not assume short-duration AF is risk-free, and do not delay emergency unstable cardioversion. ([AF guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11104284/)) |

## Valve Disorders

### Hemodynamic goals

Content version: 2026-09-27. Section ID: `valve-disorders-hemodynamic-goals`.

| Lesion | Rhythm / rate · loading · anesthesia considerations |
|---|---|
| **Aortic stenosis** | Preserve **sinus rhythm and adequate preload**; avoid tachycardia and marked bradycardia. Maintain SVR/coronary perfusion; treat hypotension promptly. Avoid abrupt vasodilation and loss of atrial contribution. ([Valve review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5613597/)) |
| **Aortic regurgitation** | Avoid bradycardia; maintain forward flow with a relatively faster rate, preserved contractility and reduced excessive SVR. Avoid sudden afterload increases; balance filling against pulmonary congestion. ([Valve review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5613597/)) |
| **Mitral stenosis** | **Slow-normal HR and sinus rhythm** favor diastolic filling. Avoid tachycardia, fluid overload and increased PVR. Hypoxia, hypercarbia, acidosis and lung overdistension can worsen pulmonary pressures/RV function. Afterload reduction does not relieve the fixed inflow obstruction. ([Valve review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5613597/)) |
| **Mitral regurgitation** | Avoid bradycardia and excessive SVR; favor forward flow, adequate contractility and a relatively faster rate. Avoid volume overload; selected hypotension may need inotropic rather than pure vasoconstrictor support. ([Valve review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5613597/)) |
| **Pulmonary hypertension / RV involvement** | Preserve oxygenation and ventilation; avoid hypoxia, hypercarbia and acidosis. Judicious preload, maintained systemic perfusion and RV support matter, especially with mitral disease. ([Valve review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5613597/)) |
| **Severe or symptomatic disease** | Confirm severity, symptoms, ventricular function and current echo; severe valve disease may need evaluation/intervention before elective surgery. These physiologic goals do not constitute clearance and must be individualized for mixed lesions. ([2024 perioperative guideline](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)) |

## WPW Syndrome

### Rhythm-Based Assessment & Treatment

Content version: 2026-09-27-r2. Section ID: `wpw-syndrome-pre-excitation-treatment`.

| Rhythm / presentation | Adult recognition · treatment |
|---|---|
| **Scope** | WPW is not synonymous with AF. Identify pulse, hemodynamic stability, regularity and QRS width before selecting treatment. Adult acute reference; see the companion Medication Dosing & Safety chart. |
| **WPW pattern: sinus rhythm** | **Short PR <120 ms, delta wave, typically widened QRS.** WPW pattern is an ECG finding; WPW syndrome includes symptomatic accessory-pathway-related tachyarrhythmia. **Do not give an acute antiarrhythmic merely for an asymptomatic ECG pattern**; arrange appropriate cardiology/EP risk assessment. ([WPW review](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK554437/); [SVT guideline](https://professional.heart.org/en/science-news/-/media/028ddd505f514ea2b3a4e74bb72e3557.ashx)) |
| **Orthodromic AVRT: stable** | Usually **regular, narrow-complex SVT**: antegrade down the AV node, retrograde up the accessory pathway. **Vagal maneuvers → adenosine 6 mg rapid IV + saline flush; 12 mg if required.** Use ECG monitoring and resuscitation readiness. If refractory/contraindicated, synchronized cardioversion or expert-directed treatment. This is **not pre-excited AF**. ([SVT guideline](https://professional.heart.org/en/science-news/-/media/028ddd505f514ea2b3a4e74bb72e3557.ashx); [2025 AHA dosing](https://www.heart.org/-/media/CPR-Files/CPR-Guidelines-Files/2025-Algorithms/Algorithm-ACLS-Tachycardia-250514.pdf)) |
| **Antidromic AVRT: stable** | Usually **regular, wide-complex tachycardia**: antegrade down the accessory pathway. Obtain expert help; **IV procainamide** is an option when appropriate. If uncertain, **treat as VT**, not presumed SVT. Do not empirically give AV-nodal blockers for suspected antidromic AVRT or an uncertain pre-excited wide-complex rhythm. ([WPW review](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK554437/)) |
| **Pre-excited AF: stable** | **Irregular, often very rapid wide-complex tachycardia with variable QRS morphology.** Expert-guided **IV procainamide OR IV ibutilide**, or electrical cardioversion. These medications are alternatives, not a routine sequential combination. Prepare for deterioration. ([AF guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11104284/); [Ibutilide label](https://labeling.pfizer.com/showlabeling.aspx?id=673)) |
| **Pre-excited AF: drugs to avoid** | **Adenosine, beta-blockers, diltiazem, verapamil, digoxin and IV amiodarone.** AV-nodal blockade may accelerate conduction over the accessory pathway and precipitate VF. This prohibition is **rhythm-specific**, not a blanket ban on adenosine for clearly identified regular narrow orthodromic AVRT. ([AF guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11104284/); [SVT guideline](https://professional.heart.org/en/science-news/-/media/028ddd505f514ea2b3a4e74bb72e3557.ashx)) |
| **Unstable tachycardia with a pulse** | Hypotension, shock, altered mental status, ischemic chest discomfort or acute HF attributable to the tachycardia: **prompt synchronized cardioversion** for AVRT/pre-excited AF. Sedate if feasible, without delaying treatment. **Do not delay unstable pre-excited AF cardioversion for a medication trial.** ([2025 AHA](https://www.heart.org/-/media/CPR-Files/CPR-Guidelines-Files/2025-Algorithms/Algorithm-ACLS-Tachycardia-250514.pdf); [AF guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11104284/)) |
| **Shock selection** | **Pre-excited AF: synchronized 200 J** under the 2025 AHA AF pathway; **regular narrow SVT: synchronized 100 J**. Follow device/local guidance; confirm synchronization and re-enable it after shocks as needed. If synchronization delays treatment in a critically unstable patient, use an **unsynchronized shock**. **Sustained polymorphic VT: unsynchronized high-energy shock**, even when a pulse is present. ([2025 AHA cardioversion](https://cpr.heart.org/-/media/CPR-Files/CPR-Guidelines-Files/2025-Algorithms/Algorithm-ACLS-Electrical-Cardioversion-250514.pdf?sc_lang=en)) |
| **Pulseless patient** | **Start CPR and follow ACLS. VF/pulseless VT: defibrillate. PEA/asystole: nonshockable.** Do not perform chest compressions solely because a patient with a pulse required an unsynchronized shock. ([2025 AHA arrest](https://cpr.heart.org/-/media/CPR-Files/CPR-Guidelines-Files/2025-Algorithms/Algorithm-ACLS-CA-250527.pdf?sc_lang=en)) |
| **After stabilization** | Obtain/review a 12-lead ECG and arrange cardiology/EP evaluation. **Accessory-pathway catheter ablation is recommended for AVRT and/or pre-excited AF.** Asymptomatic pre-excitation follows a separate risk-assessment pathway; not every ECG pattern requires urgent ablation. ([SVT guideline](https://professional.heart.org/en/science-news/-/media/028ddd505f514ea2b3a4e74bb72e3557.ashx)) |

### Medication Dosing & Safety

Content version: 2026-09-27-r2. Section ID: `wpw-syndrome-medication-dosing-safety`.

| Drug / decision | Adult dose · rhythm restriction · safety |
|---|---|
| **Scope** | Monitored adult treatment by clinicians able to manage deterioration. Select the rhythm pathway first; these drugs are not interchangeable. Verify contraindications and patient-specific dose adjustments. |
| **Adenosine: regular narrow orthodromic AVRT** | **6 mg rapid IV push followed by saline flush; 12 mg if required.** Use after vagal maneuvers in stable, clearly identified regular narrow AVRT. **Do not give for pre-excited AF or irregular wide-complex tachycardia.** A WPW history alone is not a blanket contraindication in this specific narrow, regular rhythm. ([SVT guideline](https://professional.heart.org/en/science-news/-/media/028ddd505f514ea2b3a4e74bb72e3557.ashx); [2025 AHA dosing](https://www.heart.org/-/media/CPR-Files/CPR-Guidelines-Files/2025-Algorithms/Algorithm-ACLS-Tachycardia-250514.pdf); [AF guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11104284/)) |
| **Procainamide: selected stable wide/pre-excited rhythms** | **20–50 mg/min IV**. Stop for arrhythmia suppression, hypotension, QRS widening **>50% from baseline**, or **17 mg/kg maximum**. Maintenance **1–4 mg/min** if indicated. **Avoid prolonged QT or CHF**; monitor ECG/BP and individualize for renal function. ([2025 AHA dosing](https://www.heart.org/-/media/CPR-Files/CPR-Guidelines-Files/2025-Algorithms/Algorithm-ACLS-Tachycardia-250514.pdf); [AF guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11104284/)) |
| **Ibutilide: stable pre-excited AF** | **≥60 kg: 1 mg IV over 10 min; <60 kg: 0.01 mg/kg over 10 min.** If still in the arrhythmia **10 min after the first infusion ends**, may repeat the same dose **once**. **Stop the infusion when the arrhythmia terminates, VT occurs, or QT/QTc becomes markedly prolonged.** ([AF guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11104284/); [Ibutilide label](https://labeling.pfizer.com/showlabeling.aspx?id=673)) |
| **Ibutilide: monitoring / exclusions** | Correct low **K/Mg before dosing**; avoid prolonged QT and **LVEF ≤40%**. Continuous ECG during treatment and **at least 4 h after infusion AND until QTc returns to baseline**; monitor longer for arrhythmic activity. Defibrillation/pacing capability must be available because of **torsades risk**. ([AF guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11104284/); [Ibutilide label](https://labeling.pfizer.com/showlabeling.aspx?id=673)) |
| **Do not stack QT-prolonging antiarrhythmics** | **Do not give procainamide or other class IA/III agents concurrently with ibutilide or within 4 h after ibutilide.** Review prior antiarrhythmic exposure before selecting either drug; do not automatically switch from one infusion to the other. Seek expert guidance or use electrical cardioversion when appropriate. ([Ibutilide label](https://labeling.pfizer.com/showlabeling.aspx?id=673)) |
| **Other AV-nodal blockers** | Do not apply routine AF rate-control treatment to pre-excited AF. In confirmed orthodromic AVRT, beta-blocker/diltiazem/verapamil decisions depend on resting pre-excitation and response to first-line therapy; **use expert guidance rather than a blanket WPW drug rule**. ([SVT guideline](https://professional.heart.org/en/science-news/-/media/028ddd505f514ea2b3a4e74bb72e3557.ashx); [AF guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11104284/)) |
