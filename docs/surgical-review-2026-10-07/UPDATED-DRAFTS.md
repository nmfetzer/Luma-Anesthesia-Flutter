### Obstetric airway rescue & extubation planning

- Before GA, agree on aspiration risk, anticipated airway difficulty, positioning, preoxygenation, skilled help and a failed-intubation plan. Maintain uterine displacement when applicable and use head-up/ramped positioning when suitable. ([OAA/DAS obstetric difficult-airway guideline (2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4606761/))

- Prioritize oxygenation. The OAA/DAS obstetric algorithm supports gentle mask ventilation after induction, limits repeated intubation attempts, and advises reducing or releasing cricoid pressure if it impedes ventilation or intubation. ([OAA/DAS obstetric difficult-airway guideline (2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4606761/))

- After failed intubation, declare the problem and use facemask or an appropriate second-generation supraglottic airway for oxygenation. Decide whether to wake or proceed based on airway security, aspiration risk, maternal/fetal urgency and surgical needs. ([OAA/DAS obstetric difficult-airway guideline (2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4606761/))

- A cannot-intubate/cannot-oxygenate emergency requires immediate escalation to emergency front-of-neck access under the trained local pathway; repeated unsuccessful instrumentation must not delay rescue. ([OAA/DAS obstetric difficult-airway guideline (2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4606761/))

- Plan extubation and reintubation contingencies. Awake responsiveness, satisfactory ventilation/oxygenation and adequate neuromuscular recovery are prerequisites when applicable; airway edema, traumatic intubation, pulmonary edema or ongoing instability may justify continued ventilation and ICU care rather than forced extubation. ([OAA/DAS obstetric difficult-airway guideline (2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4606761/))

### Neuraxial morphine: dose- and risk-stratified monitoring

- Assess respiratory-depression risk before selecting postoperative monitoring. Consider obesity/OSA, significant cardiopulmonary disease, chronic opioids, additional sedatives/systemic opioids, magnesium and perioperative respiratory events. ([SOAP neuraxial morphine monitoring consensus (2019 full statement)](https://www.soap.org/assets/COE/SOAP%20Consensus%20Statement%20Neuraxial%20Opioids%202019.pdf))

- For healthy low-risk patients, SOAP’s full statement considers routine institutional cesarean monitoring sufficient after intrathecal morphine at most 0.05 mg (50 micrograms) or epidural morphine at most 1 mg. This does not mean no postoperative observation. ([SOAP neuraxial morphine monitoring consensus (2019 full statement)](https://www.soap.org/assets/COE/SOAP%20Consensus%20Statement%20Neuraxial%20Opioids%202019.pdf))

- For healthy low-risk patients receiving intrathecal morphine greater than 0.05 through 0.15 mg (50–150 micrograms) or epidural morphine greater than 1 through 3 mg, the full SOAP statement recommends respiratory-rate and sedation assessment every two hours for 12 hours in addition to routine institutional observations. ([SOAP neuraxial morphine monitoring consensus (2019 full statement)](https://www.soap.org/assets/COE/SOAP%20Consensus%20Statement%20Neuraxial%20Opioids%202019.pdf))

- For higher doses, SOAP directs clinicians to ASA/ASRA monitoring: at least hourly respiratory-rate and sedation assessment for the first 12 hours, then every two hours for the next 12 hours. Higher-risk patients need individualized increased intensity and consideration of oxygenation/ventilation monitoring rather than automatic low-risk scheduling. ([SOAP neuraxial morphine monitoring consensus (2019 full statement)](https://www.soap.org/assets/COE/SOAP%20Consensus%20Statement%20Neuraxial%20Opioids%202019.pdf))

- These are monitoring dose bands, not prescribing recommendations. They apply to single-dose neuraxial morphine after cesarean, not every neuraxial opioid or continuous epidural infusion. Clinical deterioration requires immediate assessment regardless of the scheduled interval. ([SOAP neuraxial morphine monitoring consensus (2019 full statement)](https://www.soap.org/assets/COE/SOAP%20Consensus%20Statement%20Neuraxial%20Opioids%202019.pdf))

### Hemorrhage readiness & escalation

- Match vascular access and blood preparation to placental findings, anemia, bleeding risk and the clinical course. Quantify ongoing blood loss and reassess perfusion; a normal early hemoglobin does not exclude major acute bleeding. ([WHO/FIGO/ICM consolidated PPH guidelines (2025)](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/88bf11a5-93b6-4d6b-bdaa-856b46c8ed3c/content); [NICE NG192: Caesarean birth recommendations](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng192/chapter/recommendations))

- When PPH occurs, communicate and treat the cause while mobilizing the obstetric hemorrhage pathway, blood-bank support and appropriate resuscitation. Use the linked consolidated PPH guidance; do not wait for a fixed blood-loss number when the patient is deteriorating. ([WHO/FIGO/ICM consolidated PPH guidelines (2025)](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/88bf11a5-93b6-4d6b-bdaa-856b46c8ed3c/content); [NICE NG192: Caesarean birth recommendations](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng192/chapter/recommendations))

- TXA for treatment of established PPH and routine prophylactic TXA are different decisions. WHO 2025 recommends early IV treatment within three hours of birth but does not recommend routine prophylactic TXA at cesarean birth. ([WHO/FIGO/ICM consolidated PPH guidelines (2025)](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/88bf11a5-93b6-4d6b-bdaa-856b46c8ed3c/content); [NICE NG192: Caesarean birth recommendations](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng192/chapter/recommendations))

### Quick clinical overview

- Evaluate postpartum headache rather than assuming every headache after neuraxial care is PDPH; focal deficits, visual change, seizures or altered consciousness require assessment for other diagnoses. ([Multisociety PDPH guideline (2024)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/49/7/471); [SOAP obstetric thrombocytopenia consensus (2021)](https://www.soap.org/assets/docs/Consensus/SOAP%20Consensus%20Statement%20Thrombocytopenia%202021.pdf); [ASRA antithrombotic guideline, fifth edition (2025)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/early/2025/01/21/rapm-2024-105766))

- Consider therapeutic EBP for PDPH that remains disabling despite conservative care, or for significant neurological sequelae after appropriate assessment. Prophylactic EBP is not routine. ([Multisociety PDPH guideline (2024)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/49/7/471); [SOAP obstetric thrombocytopenia consensus (2021)](https://www.soap.org/assets/docs/Consensus/SOAP%20Consensus%20Statement%20Thrombocytopenia%202021.pdf); [ASRA antithrombotic guideline, fifth edition (2025)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/early/2025/01/21/rapm-2024-105766))

- Check infection, hemostasis and antithrombotic exposure. Use asepsis, individualized localization and slow incremental injection; stop for significant pain or neurological symptoms. ([Multisociety PDPH guideline (2024)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/49/7/471); [SOAP obstetric thrombocytopenia consensus (2021)](https://www.soap.org/assets/docs/Consensus/SOAP%20Consensus%20Statement%20Thrombocytopenia%202021.pdf); [ASRA antithrombotic guideline, fifth edition (2025)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/early/2025/01/21/rapm-2024-105766))

- Assess response and provide written return precautions, contact details and follow-up until resolution. Worsening, changed or neurologically complicated headache after EBP needs urgent reassessment, not automatic repeat patching. ([Multisociety PDPH guideline (2024)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/49/7/471); [SOAP obstetric thrombocytopenia consensus (2021)](https://www.soap.org/assets/docs/Consensus/SOAP%20Consensus%20Statement%20Thrombocytopenia%202021.pdf); [ASRA antithrombotic guideline, fifth edition (2025)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/early/2025/01/21/rapm-2024-105766))

### Indication, level & imaging guidance

- Therapeutic EBP places autologous blood in the epidural space for a suspected dural leak. When the puncture level is known, the multisociety guideline favors that level or one interspace below. ([Multisociety PDPH guideline (2024)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/49/7/471))

- Radiological guidance is individualized to anatomy, prior surgery, failed prior attempts and operator expertise; routine fluoroscopy, contrast or prone positioning is not established as mandatory for a standard obstetric interlaminar patch. ([Multisociety PDPH guideline (2024)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/49/7/471))

- An EBP performed within 48 hours may be more likely to require repetition. Discuss this uncertainty without treating 48 hours as a mandatory delay when symptoms warrant earlier treatment. ([Multisociety PDPH guideline (2024)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/49/7/471))

### Headache differential & contraindications

- Review onset, orthostatic character, neuraxial history, blood pressure, neurological examination, fever and associated symptoms. Consider preeclampsia/PRES, cerebral venous thrombosis, subdural hematoma and infection when the presentation is atypical. ([Multisociety PDPH guideline (2024)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/49/7/471))

- Focal neurological deficits, visual changes, altered consciousness or seizures should prompt neuroimaging for alternative diagnoses. Consider imaging for nonorthostatic headache, a change from orthostatic to nonorthostatic pain, or onset more than five days after suspected puncture. ([Multisociety PDPH guideline (2024)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/49/7/471))

- Routine cranial imaging is not required to establish typical PDPH; normal imaging does not exclude it. Select MRI, CT and venous imaging according to the suspected diagnosis, urgency and availability. ([Multisociety PDPH guideline (2024)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/49/7/471))

- Do not perform EBP in systemic infection. Assess puncture-site infection, coagulation disorder and recent anticoagulant exposure; obtain consent covering failure, repeat patching, back pain and uncommon neurological complications. ([Multisociety PDPH guideline (2024)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/49/7/471))

### Asepsis, positioning & observation

- Use strict aseptic technique for blood collection and epidural access. Choose a position and localization method that permit safe access, patient tolerance and communication; no single position is mandated by the multisociety guideline. ([Multisociety PDPH guideline (2024)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/49/7/471))

- Agree on observation, rescue capability and postprocedure assessment appropriate to the patient and any sedation. Document baseline neurological symptoms and a plan to reassess headache and neurological function. ([Multisociety PDPH guideline (2024)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/49/7/471))

### Sedation & communication

- EBP does not itself require general anesthesia, intubation or neuromuscular blockade. If sedation is considered, assess its risks separately and preserve the ability to report pain or neurological symptoms during injection. ([Multisociety PDPH guideline (2024)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/49/7/471); [OAA/DAS obstetric difficult-airway guideline (2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4606761/))

- The multisociety guideline does not establish a universal sedative regimen. Sedation requires appropriate monitoring and airway-rescue capability; local anesthesia and communication should not be replaced by an unsupported deep-sedation recipe. ([Multisociety PDPH guideline (2024)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/49/7/471); [OAA/DAS obstetric difficult-airway guideline (2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4606761/))

### Slow incremental blood injection

- The optimal volume is uncertain; the multisociety guideline notes commonly recommended adult lumbar volumes of 15–20 mL. This is not a mandatory volume to reach. ([Multisociety PDPH guideline (2024)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/49/7/471))

- Inject slowly and incrementally. Stop for significant backache, headache, pressure paresthesia or neurological symptoms; reassess before any decision to continue. ([Multisociety PDPH guideline (2024)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/49/7/471))

- Volumes greater than 30 mL have not shown better success. Do not pursue a volume target despite poor tolerance or use success estimates as a promise to the patient. ([Multisociety PDPH guideline (2024)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/49/7/471))

### Failed patch, changed headache & neurological red flags

- Persistent or recurrent PDPH may warrant another EBP after reassessment. If the first patch has no effect, the diagnosis is uncertain or the headache changes, evaluate alternative causes and consider specialist input before repetition. ([Multisociety PDPH guideline (2024)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/49/7/471))

- Worsening symptoms despite EBP, new focal neurological findings or a change in headache character warrant urgent neuroimaging and appropriate specialist referral. ([Multisociety PDPH guideline (2024)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/49/7/471))

- New severe back pain, weakness, sensory changes or bladder/bowel dysfunction should not be dismissed as routine postpatch discomfort; urgently assess for neurological complications. ([Multisociety PDPH guideline (2024)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/49/7/471))

- Changed headache, including a recumbency-worsened pattern, requires reassessment rather than an automatic assumption of persistent CSF leak. ([Multisociety PDPH guideline (2024)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/49/7/471))

### Hydration rather than a fixed fluid prescription

- Maintain adequate hydration; routine forced fluid loading is not an established treatment for a dural leak. Use intravenous fluid when oral intake is inadequate or another clinical indication exists. ([Multisociety PDPH guideline (2024)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/49/7/471))

- Autologous blood used for EBP is not a transfusion strategy. Fluid and cardiovascular management remain individualized. ([Multisociety PDPH guideline (2024)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/49/7/471))

### Conservative care & patient counseling

- Use appropriate simple analgesia and consider caffeine within the guideline’s patient-specific limits while evaluating symptoms. Conservative care must not delay evaluation of red flags or appropriate EBP for disabling symptoms. ([Multisociety PDPH guideline (2024)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/49/7/471))

- Explain that repeat treatment may be needed and that aftercare restrictions have limited supporting evidence. Do not describe a fixed activity restriction period as proven to prevent recurrence. ([Multisociety PDPH guideline (2024)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/49/7/471))

### Postprocedure assessment & follow-up

- Reassess headache response, back symptoms and neurological status before discharge. Supine observation is commonly used, but evidence is insufficient to mandate one immobilization duration for all patients. ([Multisociety PDPH guideline (2024)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/49/7/471))

- Provide verbal and written warning signs, contact details and a follow-up plan until headache resolves. Communicate the diagnosis and treatment to the obstetric team and relevant outpatient clinicians. ([Multisociety PDPH guideline (2024)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/49/7/471))

- Urgent return is needed for worsening or changed headache, focal neurological symptoms, seizures, altered consciousness or other concerning findings. Recovery from any sedation must also be adequate for discharge. ([Multisociety PDPH guideline (2024)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/49/7/471))

### Platelets & antithrombotic eligibility

- For obstetric neuraxial procedures, SOAP describes likely low hematoma risk at platelets at least 70 × 10⁹/L (70,000/µL) in gestational thrombocytopenia, ITP or hypertensive disorders of pregnancy when additional risk factors, bleeding history and DIC concerns are absent. This is a qualified risk framework, not an automatic EBP clearance threshold. ([Multisociety PDPH guideline (2024)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/49/7/471); [SOAP obstetric thrombocytopenia consensus (2021)](https://www.soap.org/assets/docs/Consensus/SOAP%20Consensus%20Statement%20Thrombocytopenia%202021.pdf); [ASRA antithrombotic guideline, fifth edition (2025)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/early/2025/01/21/rapm-2024-105766))

- Assess platelet trend, etiology, platelet function concerns, bleeding history and concurrent antithrombotics. Unknown etiology, lower counts or suspected coagulopathy require individualized expert assessment rather than substituting an older review author’s preferred threshold. ([Multisociety PDPH guideline (2024)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/49/7/471); [SOAP obstetric thrombocytopenia consensus (2021)](https://www.soap.org/assets/docs/Consensus/SOAP%20Consensus%20Statement%20Thrombocytopenia%202021.pdf); [ASRA antithrombotic guideline, fifth edition (2025)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/early/2025/01/21/rapm-2024-105766))

- For suspected HELLP, a platelet count within six hours of the planned neuraxial procedure may be reasonable; do not generalize this interval to every patient with preeclampsia. ([Multisociety PDPH guideline (2024)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/49/7/471); [SOAP obstetric thrombocytopenia consensus (2021)](https://www.soap.org/assets/docs/Consensus/SOAP%20Consensus%20Statement%20Thrombocytopenia%202021.pdf); [ASRA antithrombotic guideline, fifth edition (2025)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/early/2025/01/21/rapm-2024-105766))

- Apply the current drug-, dose- and renal-function-specific neuraxial interruption/restart guidance before EBP. Do not independently stop anticoagulation without assessing its indication and thrombosis risk. ([Multisociety PDPH guideline (2024)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/49/7/471); [SOAP obstetric thrombocytopenia consensus (2021)](https://www.soap.org/assets/docs/Consensus/SOAP%20Consensus%20Statement%20Thrombocytopenia%202021.pdf); [ASRA antithrombotic guideline, fifth edition (2025)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/early/2025/01/21/rapm-2024-105766))

### Evidence scope & limitations

- The 2024 multisociety PDPH guideline now supplies the principal diagnostic, EBP and follow-up framework. Many recommendations still have low or moderate certainty; individualized clinical judgment remains necessary. ([Multisociety PDPH guideline (2024)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/49/7/471); [SOAP obstetric thrombocytopenia consensus (2021)](https://www.soap.org/assets/docs/Consensus/SOAP%20Consensus%20Statement%20Thrombocytopenia%202021.pdf); [ASRA antithrombotic guideline, fifth edition (2025)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/early/2025/01/21/rapm-2024-105766))

- SOAP platelet guidance and ASRA antithrombotic guidance supplement EBP risk assessment. Neither establishes zero risk or an automatic numerical clearance rule. ([Multisociety PDPH guideline (2024)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/49/7/471); [SOAP obstetric thrombocytopenia consensus (2021)](https://www.soap.org/assets/docs/Consensus/SOAP%20Consensus%20Statement%20Thrombocytopenia%202021.pdf); [ASRA antithrombotic guideline, fifth edition (2025)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/early/2025/01/21/rapm-2024-105766))

- The previously identified retracted PDPH review is not used to support the revised content. Source reconciliation is not independent clinical approval. ([Multisociety PDPH guideline (2024)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/49/7/471); [SOAP obstetric thrombocytopenia consensus (2021)](https://www.soap.org/assets/docs/Consensus/SOAP%20Consensus%20Statement%20Thrombocytopenia%202021.pdf); [ASRA antithrombotic guideline, fifth edition (2025)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/early/2025/01/21/rapm-2024-105766))

## High-Risk Obstetric Anesthesia — Cardiac Disease in Pregnancy

- Add usable obstetric airway/escalation and recovery framework; retain individualized clinical decisions.

### Lesion-specific postpartum surveillance

- Specify the monitoring location, rhythm surveillance, fluid balance, heart-failure warning signs and anticoagulation restart plan before delivery; update for actual obstetric and cardiac events. ([ESC cardiovascular disease and pregnancy guideline (2025)](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/46/43/4462/8234487?login=false))

- Do not apply a fixed ICU duration to all cardiac conditions. Pulmonary arterial hypertension is an important exception to casual low-acuity recovery: ESC recommends intensive-care monitoring during and after delivery because postpartum risk is particularly high. ([ESC cardiovascular disease and pregnancy guideline (2025)](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/46/43/4462/8234487?login=false))

- Other congenital, valvular or myocardial disease requires surveillance proportionate to severity, arrhythmias, ventricular function and the delivery course. Anticipate postpartum congestion and coordinate breastfeeding and medication planning with the specialist team. ([ESC cardiovascular disease and pregnancy guideline (2025)](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/46/43/4462/8234487?login=false))

Maintain uterine displacement when applicable and use head-up/ramped positioning when suitable. - Prioritize oxygenation. The OAA/DAS obstetric algorithm supports gentle mask ventilation after induction, limits repeated intubation attempts, and advises reducing or releasing cricoid pressure if it impedes ventilation or intubation. ([OAA/DAS obstetric difficult-airway guideline (2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4606761/))

- After failed intubation, declare the problem and use facemask or an appropriate second-generation supraglottic airway for oxygenation. Decide whether to wake or proceed based on airway security, aspiration risk, maternal/fetal urgency and surgical needs. ([OAA/DAS obstetric difficult-airway guideline (2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4606761/))

([OAA/DAS obstetric difficult-airway guideline (2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4606761/))

- Plan extubation and reintubation contingencies. Awake responsiveness, satisfactory ventilation/oxygenation and adequate neuromuscular recovery are prerequisites when applicable; airway edema, traumatic intubation, pulmonary edema or ongoing instability may justify continued ventilation and ICU care rather than forced extubation. ### Lesion-specific delivery & anesthesia distinctions

- Use a documented Pregnancy Heart Team plan for significant disease, including lesion severity, ventricular/RV function, anticoagulation, delivery approach, rescue capability and postpartum surveillance. The 2025 ESC guideline uses the updated mWHO 2.0 framework. ### Uterotonics: cardiac lesion & hemorrhage context

- Agree the hemorrhage and uterotonic plan before delivery. ([ESC cardiovascular disease and pregnancy guideline (2025)](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/46/43/4462/8234487?login=false); [Obstetric Anesthesia and Heart Disease: Practical Clinical Considerations (2021)](https://pubs.asahq.org/anesthesiology/article/135/1/164/115827/Obstetric-Anesthesia-and-Heart-Disease-Practical))

([ESC cardiovascular disease and pregnancy guideline (2025)](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/46/43/4462/8234487?login=false); [Obstetric Anesthesia and Heart Disease: Practical Clinical Considerations (2021)](https://pubs.asahq.org/anesthesiology/article/135/1/164/115827/Obstetric-Anesthesia-and-Heart-Disease-Practical))

### Antithrombotics: placement, removal & restart

- Document the exact anticoagulant, dose, indication, last administration, renal function, other hemostasis-altering drugs and planned postpartum restart. Catheter removal is also a neuraxial bleeding-risk event. ([ASRA antithrombotic guideline, fifth edition (2025)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/early/2025/01/21/rapm-2024-105766))

- ASRA fifth-edition minimum preprocedure intervals for LMWH are at least 12 hours after low-dose LMWH and at least 24 hours after high/therapeutic-dose LMWH. Residual effect may persist longer, especially with renal impairment; these intervals are not automatic clearance. ([ASRA antithrombotic guideline, fifth edition (2025)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/early/2025/01/21/rapm-2024-105766))

- For postoperative twice-daily low-dose LMWH, remove the catheter before starting LMWH; the first dose is the following day and at least 12 hours after placement, and at least four hours after removal. ([ASRA antithrombotic guideline, fifth edition (2025)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/early/2025/01/21/rapm-2024-105766))

- For postoperative once-daily low-dose LMWH, the first dose is at least 12 hours after placement. If a catheter is maintained, avoid additional hemostasis-altering medications; remove it at least 12 hours after the last LMWH dose and wait at least four hours before the next dose. ([ASRA antithrombotic guideline, fifth edition (2025)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/early/2025/01/21/rapm-2024-105766))

- For postoperative high-dose LMWH, satisfy both surgical-hemostasis and neuraxial timing requirements: ASRA describes restart at 24 hours after non-high-bleeding-risk surgery or 48–72 hours after high-bleeding-risk surgery, with catheter removal at least four hours before the first dose and that dose at least 24 hours after placement. ([ASRA antithrombotic guideline, fifth edition (2025)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/early/2025/01/21/rapm-2024-105766))

- This is a limited LMWH reference, not a complete anticoagulant table. Use the linked guideline for UFH, DOACs, antiplatelets, traumatic placement, assays and renal impairment. Urgent maternal/fetal situations require multidisciplinary risk-benefit decisions rather than automatic extrapolation. ([ASRA antithrombotic guideline, fifth edition (2025)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/early/2025/01/21/rapm-2024-105766))

### Suspected LAST: rescue reference

- For seizure, arrhythmia or cardiovascular instability concerning for LAST after local anesthetic exposure, stop administration, call for help and obtain the LAST kit and ASRA checklist. Support airway/oxygenation and consider lipid emulsion early. ([ASRA LAST checklist (2020 version)](https://asra.com/docs/default-source/guidelines-articles/local-anesthetic-systemic-toxicity-rgb.pdf?sfvrsn=33b348e_2); [StatPearls: Lipid Emulsion Therapy](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK549897/))

- Use 20% lipid emulsion, not propofol as a lipid substitute. ASRA’s checklist gives approximately 1.5 mL/kg over 2–3 minutes followed by 0.25 mL/kg/min for patients under 70 kg; for patients over 70 kg it gives approximately 100 mL over 2–3 minutes followed by approximately 250 mL over 15–20 minutes. ([ASRA LAST checklist (2020 version)](https://asra.com/docs/default-source/guidelines-articles/local-anesthetic-systemic-toxicity-rgb.pdf?sfvrsn=33b348e_2); [StatPearls: Lipid Emulsion Therapy](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK549897/))

- If instability persists, the checklist allows repeat bolus and doubling the infusion. Continue lipid for more than 15 minutes after hemodynamic stability; maximum cumulative lipid dose is 12 mL/kg. ([ASRA LAST checklist (2020 version)](https://asra.com/docs/default-source/guidelines-articles/local-anesthetic-systemic-toxicity-rgb.pdf?sfvrsn=33b348e_2); [StatPearls: Lipid Emulsion Therapy](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK549897/))

- LAST resuscitation differs from standard ACLS: benzodiazepines are preferred for seizures; if epinephrine is needed, start with a smaller dose, less than 1 microgram/kg. Avoid additional local anesthetics, beta blockers, calcium-channel blockers and vasopressin. ([ASRA LAST checklist (2020 version)](https://asra.com/docs/default-source/guidelines-articles/local-anesthetic-systemic-toxicity-rgb.pdf?sfvrsn=33b348e_2); [StatPearls: Lipid Emulsion Therapy](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK549897/))

- After stabilization, ASRA describes observation for at least two hours after seizure, four to six hours after cardiovascular instability, and individualized longer care after cardiac arrest. Maternal/fetal status and the overall obstetric course may require additional monitoring. ([ASRA LAST checklist (2020 version)](https://asra.com/docs/default-source/guidelines-articles/local-anesthetic-systemic-toxicity-rgb.pdf?sfvrsn=33b348e_2); [StatPearls: Lipid Emulsion Therapy](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK549897/))

- Add usable obstetric airway/escalation and recovery framework; retain individualized clinical decisions.

### Procedure & approach

- PAS surgery should be planned in a tertiary service with an experienced multidisciplinary team; consent should address transfusion, urinary or intestinal injury, fistula, and possible hysterectomy. ([Management of placenta accreta spectrum](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10183858/); [RCOG: Placenta Praevia and Placenta Accreta Spectrum (2026)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13485456/))

- Plan delivery timing with the PAS multidisciplinary team using maternal symptoms, bleeding, placental findings, preterm-birth risk and applicable current guidance. The earlier cited 34+0–35+6-week window is not a universal rule for every stable patient; do not delay indicated urgent delivery to reach a planned gestation. ([Management of placenta accreta spectrum](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10183858/); [RCOG: Placenta Praevia and Placenta Accreta Spectrum (2026)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13485456/))

- Perform hysterotomy outside the invaded area, extract the fetus, and keep the placenta in situ. If hysterectomy is undertaken, remove the uterus with the placenta in situ rather than separating it. ([Management of placenta accreta spectrum](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10183858/); [RCOG: Placenta Praevia and Placenta Accreta Spectrum (2026)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13485456/))

### Anesthetic choice & planned or urgent conversion

- Discuss GA, neuraxial anesthesia and planned sequential neuraxial-to-GA options before surgery. The 2026 RCOG guidance supports individualized selection with consent for possible conversion; PAS alone does not mandate one technique. ([RCOG: Placenta Praevia and Placenta Accreta Spectrum (2026)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13485456/); [OAA/DAS obstetric difficult-airway guideline (2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4606761/))

- Plan how prolonged surgery, major bleeding, hemodynamic deterioration or inadequate anesthesia would trigger reassessment and conversion. Airway access and induction during evolving shock must be considered before the crisis. ([RCOG: Placenta Praevia and Placenta Accreta Spectrum (2026)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13485456/); [OAA/DAS obstetric difficult-airway guideline (2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4606761/))

- Neuraxial-to-GA after delivery can be a planned strategy, not a compulsory step. The obstetric airway rescue section supplies the shared framework; anticipated complexity may require additional specialist planning. ([RCOG: Placenta Praevia and Placenta Accreta Spectrum (2026)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13485456/); [OAA/DAS obstetric difficult-airway guideline (2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4606761/))

### Hemorrhage, injury & postoperative escalation

- For unexpected PAS, avoid unplanned placental disruption, call for experienced support and reassess the surgical, anesthetic and hemorrhage plan. Major bleeding and adjacent-organ injury require coordinated intervention. ([RCOG: Placenta Praevia and Placenta Accreta Spectrum (2026)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13485456/))

- Prepare immediate blood-product access and adult critical-care capability. Postoperative level of care depends on bleeding, organ dysfunction, respiratory status and support requirements; not every stable patient automatically requires ongoing ventilation or vasopressors. ([RCOG: Placenta Praevia and Placenta Accreta Spectrum (2026)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13485456/))

- Conservative management with retained placenta requires specialist follow-up and emergency access for delayed bleeding or infection. Routine cesarean discharge instructions alone are insufficient. ([RCOG: Placenta Praevia and Placenta Accreta Spectrum (2026)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13485456/))

### Recovery, pain & disposition

- Determine extubation versus continued ventilation from airway safety, gas exchange, hemodynamics, hemostasis, temperature and the support required. Use the obstetric airway section for edema/difficult-reintubation considerations. ([RCOG: Placenta Praevia and Placenta Accreta Spectrum (2026)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13485456/); [OAA/DAS obstetric difficult-airway guideline (2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4606761/); [ASRA antithrombotic guideline, fifth edition (2025)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/early/2025/01/21/rapm-2024-105766))

- Communicate blood loss/transfusion, coagulation trends, surgical concerns, vascular adjuncts and analgesic plan to the receiving team. Plan surveillance for ongoing or concealed bleeding. ([RCOG: Placenta Praevia and Placenta Accreta Spectrum (2026)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13485456/); [OAA/DAS obstetric difficult-airway guideline (2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4606761/); [ASRA antithrombotic guideline, fifth edition (2025)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/early/2025/01/21/rapm-2024-105766))

- Discuss postoperative pain control and psychological support as part of specialist PAS care. Individualize multimodal analgesia to organ function, bleeding and anticoagulation; catheter removal requires a fresh hemostasis/timing assessment. ([RCOG: Placenta Praevia and Placenta Accreta Spectrum (2026)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13485456/); [OAA/DAS obstetric difficult-airway guideline (2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4606761/); [ASRA antithrombotic guideline, fifth edition (2025)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/early/2025/01/21/rapm-2024-105766))

### Obstetric airway rescue & extubation planning

- Before GA, agree on aspiration risk, anticipated airway difficulty, positioning, preoxygenation, skilled help and a failed-intubation plan. Maintain uterine displacement when applicable and use head-up/ramped positioning when suitable. ([OAA/DAS obstetric difficult-airway guideline (2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4606761/))

- Prioritize oxygenation. The OAA/DAS obstetric algorithm supports gentle mask ventilation after induction, limits repeated intubation attempts, and advises reducing or releasing cricoid pressure if it impedes ventilation or intubation. ([OAA/DAS obstetric difficult-airway guideline (2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4606761/))

- After failed intubation, declare the problem and use facemask or an appropriate second-generation supraglottic airway for oxygenation. Decide whether to wake or proceed based on airway security, aspiration risk, maternal/fetal urgency and surgical needs. ([OAA/DAS obstetric difficult-airway guideline (2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4606761/))

- A cannot-intubate/cannot-oxygenate emergency requires immediate escalation to emergency front-of-neck access under the trained local pathway; repeated unsuccessful instrumentation must not delay rescue. ([OAA/DAS obstetric difficult-airway guideline (2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4606761/))

- Plan extubation and reintubation contingencies. Awake responsiveness, satisfactory ventilation/oxygenation and adequate neuromuscular recovery are prerequisites when applicable; airway edema, traumatic intubation, pulmonary edema or ongoing instability may justify continued ventilation and ICU care rather than forced extubation. ([OAA/DAS obstetric difficult-airway guideline (2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4606761/))

### Vascular adjuncts & evidence limits

- Do not present prophylactic arterial balloons or other interventional-radiology procedures as routine PAS care. The 2026 RCOG guideline finds insufficient evidence for routine use by an expert surgical team. ([RCOG: Placenta Praevia and Placenta Accreta Spectrum (2026)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13485456/))

- If an adjunct is selected, agree on access, thrombosis/ischemia surveillance, anticoagulation and rescue. Evidence varies with PAS severity, intervention and surgical approach; an adjunct cannot replace blood availability and experienced multidisciplinary care. ([RCOG: Placenta Praevia and Placenta Accreta Spectrum (2026)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13485456/))

### Planned-delivery timing: source discrepancy

- The accessed 2026 RCOG full text is internally inconsistent for high-probability PAS without preterm-birth risk factors or antenatal bleeding: its key recommendations state 36+1–37+0 weeks, while section 10.2 states 35+0–36+6 weeks. ([RCOG: Placenta Praevia and Placenta Accreta Spectrum (2026)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13485456/))

- The same section discusses an ACOG 34+0–35+6-week approach when significant blood loss and cesarean hysterectomy are anticipated. These statements must not be merged into one supposedly universal recommendation. ([RCOG: Placenta Praevia and Placenta Accreta Spectrum (2026)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13485456/))

- Confirm the authoritative recommendation and any correction with the obstetric/PAS service before approving a new timing instruction. This discrepancy is an explicit review hold on timing, not evidence to postpone care in a patient with bleeding, labor or another urgent indication. ([RCOG: Placenta Praevia and Placenta Accreta Spectrum (2026)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13485456/))

### Cause-directed treatment & early TXA

- Resuscitation and bleeding control proceed together. Evaluate uterine tone, tissue, trauma and coagulation while mobilizing senior obstetric/anesthesia support and the hemorrhage pathway. ([WHO/FIGO/ICM consolidated PPH guidelines (2025)](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/88bf11a5-93b6-4d6b-bdaa-856b46c8ed3c/content))

- WHO 2025 recommends IV oxytocin as first-line uterotonic treatment, with further uterotonics selected for response, contraindications and availability. Avoid rapid unopposed IV oxytocin administration; use the appropriate institutional obstetric regimen. ([WHO/FIGO/ICM consolidated PPH guidelines (2025)](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/88bf11a5-93b6-4d6b-bdaa-856b46c8ed3c/content))

- For established PPH after vaginal or cesarean birth, give TXA as early as possible within three hours of birth: WHO describes 1 g IV over 10 minutes, with a second 1 g IV if bleeding continues after 30 minutes or restarts within 24 hours of completing the first dose. ([WHO/FIGO/ICM consolidated PPH guidelines (2025)](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/88bf11a5-93b6-4d6b-bdaa-856b46c8ed3c/content))

- The three-hour window is measured from birth, not recognition of bleeding. WHO does not support starting TXA after that window for PPH; assess contraindications, including a known thromboembolic event during pregnancy. TXA is an antifibrinolytic, not a uterotonic, and this recommendation is IV only. ([WHO/FIGO/ICM consolidated PPH guidelines (2025)](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/88bf11a5-93b6-4d6b-bdaa-856b46c8ed3c/content))

- WHO’s first-response bundle combines uterine massage, oxytocic medication, TXA, IV fluid, genital-tract examination and escalation. Its formal bundle recommendation is for vaginal birth; individual treatment interventions also apply to cesarean PPH. Do not mislabel bundle trial evidence as cesarean-specific. ([WHO/FIGO/ICM consolidated PPH guidelines (2025)](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/88bf11a5-93b6-4d6b-bdaa-856b46c8ed3c/content))

### Resuscitation & hemostatic therapy

- Activate the local MTP when indicated and reassess clinical perfusion, ongoing loss and laboratory/viscoelastic results. Do not wait for a low hemoglobin in rapid bleeding; early values may be falsely reassuring. ([WHO/FIGO/ICM consolidated PPH guidelines (2025)](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/88bf11a5-93b6-4d6b-bdaa-856b46c8ed3c/content); [Management of major obstetric haemorrhage](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6144554/))

- WHO favors isotonic crystalloids over colloids for initial IV resuscitation, while avoiding overload, especially in cardiac disease or preeclampsia. Fluids do not replace definitive bleeding control or needed blood components. ([WHO/FIGO/ICM consolidated PPH guidelines (2025)](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/88bf11a5-93b6-4d6b-bdaa-856b46c8ed3c/content); [Management of major obstetric haemorrhage](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6144554/))

- Do not impose one fixed RBC:plasma ratio on every PPH. Use the institution’s validated emergency-release and transfusion pathway, then targeted therapy as results become available. ([WHO/FIGO/ICM consolidated PPH guidelines (2025)](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/88bf11a5-93b6-4d6b-bdaa-856b46c8ed3c/content); [Management of major obstetric haemorrhage](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6144554/))

- Assess fibrinogen early. WHO describes replacement when below 2 g/L with a therapeutic goal at least 2 g/L; product choice, dosing and reassessment depend on available concentrate/cryoprecipitate and the local protocol. ([WHO/FIGO/ICM consolidated PPH guidelines (2025)](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/88bf11a5-93b6-4d6b-bdaa-856b46c8ed3c/content); [Management of major obstetric haemorrhage](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6144554/))

- Follow calcium, potassium, temperature and acid-base status during major transfusion. Address metabolic complications and surgical hemostasis together rather than treating every persistent bleed as a product-ratio problem. ([WHO/FIGO/ICM consolidated PPH guidelines (2025)](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/88bf11a5-93b6-4d6b-bdaa-856b46c8ed3c/content); [Management of major obstetric haemorrhage](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6144554/))

### Posthemorrhage reassessment & recovery level

- After bleeding control, reassess perfusion, ongoing loss, coagulation, temperature, electrolytes, respiratory function and support requirements. ([WHO/FIGO/ICM consolidated PPH guidelines (2025)](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/88bf11a5-93b6-4d6b-bdaa-856b46c8ed3c/content); [OAA/DAS obstetric difficult-airway guideline (2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4606761/))

### Obstetric airway rescue & extubation planning

### Neuraxial morphine: dose- and risk-stratified monitoring

- Assess respiratory-depression risk before selecting postoperative monitoring. ([SOAP neuraxial morphine monitoring consensus (2019 full statement)](https://www.soap.org/assets/COE/SOAP%20Consensus%20Statement%20Neuraxial%20Opioids%202019.pdf))

- For healthy low-risk patients receiving intrathecal morphine greater than 0.05 through 0.15 mg (50–150 micrograms) or epidural morphine greater than 1 through 3 mg, the full SOAP statement recommends respiratory-rate and sedation assessment every two hours for 12 hours in addition to routine institutional observations. - Add current antithrombotic placement/removal/restart reference without a universal table.
- Reconcile evidence notes with focused supplementation; do not claim full case approval.

### Cementation: team preparation & vigilance

- Before femoral-canal instrumentation and cement insertion, identify heightened cardiopulmonary risk, agree team roles and ensure the surgeon’s warning is heard and acknowledged. Have vasoactive support immediately available and correct hypovolemia without indiscriminate fluid loading. ([AAGBI/BOA/BGS cemented hemiarthroplasty safety guideline (2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6681143/))

- The 2015 joint safety guideline for cemented hemiarthroplasty calls for close blood-pressure monitoring during and shortly after cementation, with invasive monitoring in higher-risk patients. A sudden fall in pressure or end-tidal CO₂ under GA may signal right-heart failure or a major fall in cardiac output. ([AAGBI/BOA/BGS cemented hemiarthroplasty safety guideline (2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6681143/))

- That guideline advises systolic pressure within 20% of the pre-induction value in its setting. This is source-specific guidance, not a universal target for all arthroplasty patients or a substitute for assessing perfusion and the patient’s baseline. ([AAGBI/BOA/BGS cemented hemiarthroplasty safety guideline (2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6681143/))

- New hypoxemia, hypotension or cardiovascular collapse around instrumentation, cementation or prosthesis insertion requires immediate communication, resuscitation and evaluation for BCIS and other causes. The prevention guideline is not a complete stand-alone cardiac-arrest algorithm. ([AAGBI/BOA/BGS cemented hemiarthroplasty safety guideline (2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6681143/))

- The guideline’s direct focus is cemented hemiarthroplasty for hip fracture. Applying its team-preparation principles to other cemented arthroplasty requires procedural and patient context; it does not choose the implant or mandate uncemented surgery. ([AAGBI/BOA/BGS cemented hemiarthroplasty safety guideline (2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6681143/))

### Neuraxial/deep-block & thromboprophylaxis coordination

- Before neuraxial or deep plexus/peripheral procedures, identify the exact anticoagulant or antiplatelet drug, dose, last administration, renal function and additional bleeding risks. Use current ASRA drug-specific guidance rather than a single interval for every agent. ([ASRA antithrombotic guideline, fifth edition (2025)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/early/2025/01/21/rapm-2024-105766))

- Coordinate catheter removal and the first postoperative anticoagulant dose before handoff. Surgical hemostasis, dose intensity and neuraxial timing all matter; catheter removal is itself a bleeding-risk event. ([ASRA antithrombotic guideline, fifth edition (2025)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/early/2025/01/21/rapm-2024-105766))

- Do not stop clinically indicated antithrombotic treatment independently or delay urgent surgery solely to obtain a preferred anesthetic technique. Discuss feasible anesthetic alternatives and the bleeding/thrombosis tradeoff with the treating team. ([ASRA antithrombotic guideline, fifth edition (2025)](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/early/2025/01/21/rapm-2024-105766))

## Long Bone ORIF (Tibia / Femur / Humerus)

- Add lower-leg-specific regional and compartment-syndrome safeguards.
- Reconcile evidence notes with focused supplementation; do not claim full case approval.

### Lower-leg trauma: analgesia & compartment surveillance

- For tibial/lower-leg injury, identify acute compartment syndrome risk and agree a multidisciplinary surveillance and escalation plan. Observations should occur at defined intervals by trained staff, with equipment and a pathway for compartment-pressure measurement when indicated. ([Association of Anaesthetists: lower-leg trauma regional analgesia and ACS (2021)](https://ra-uk.org/media/xbwbsr5c/regional_analgesia_for_lower_leg_trauma_and_the_risk_of_acute_compartment_syndrome.pdf))

- Offer effective analgesia; regional analgesia is not universally prohibited. The 2021 guideline advises avoiding dense regional blocks whose duration substantially exceeds surgery and describes lower-concentration peripheral blocks without adjuncts as an option only with appropriate, effective surveillance. ([Association of Anaesthetists: lower-leg trauma regional analgesia and ACS (2021)](https://ra-uk.org/media/xbwbsr5c/regional_analgesia_for_lower_leg_trauma_and_the_risk_of_acute_compartment_syndrome.pdf))

- Increasing pain or pain on passive stretch can be concerning, but severe pain is not always present. Do not wait for pulselessness or paralysis, which are late signs, or use apparent comfort under a block to exclude compartment syndrome. ([Association of Anaesthetists: lower-leg trauma regional analgesia and ACS (2021)](https://ra-uk.org/media/xbwbsr5c/regional_analgesia_for_lower_leg_trauma_and_the_risk_of_acute_compartment_syndrome.pdf))

- When the examination is unreliable or the diagnosis remains uncertain, obtain urgent surgical assessment and use compartment-pressure measurement as appropriate. No single sign or test guarantees exclusion. ([Association of Anaesthetists: lower-leg trauma regional analgesia and ACS (2021)](https://ra-uk.org/media/xbwbsr5c/regional_analgesia_for_lower_leg_trauma_and_the_risk_of_acute_compartment_syndrome.pdf))

- The guideline is specifically about lower-leg trauma and rests largely on limited observational evidence and consensus. It does not validate a universal block regimen for humeral or femoral fractures; local surveillance capability and the actual injury matter. ([Association of Anaesthetists: lower-leg trauma regional analgesia and ACS (2021)](https://ra-uk.org/media/xbwbsr5c/regional_analgesia_for_lower_leg_trauma_and_the_risk_of_acute_compartment_syndrome.pdf))

## Rotator Cuff Repair (Arthroscopic)

- Add brain-level pressure interpretation without a universal target.
- Add explicit pre-extubation evaluation and qualified postoperative airway plan.
- Reconcile evidence notes with focused supplementation; do not claim full case approval.

### Beach-chair position: pressure at the brain

- When the head is above the arm cuff, arm pressure overestimates pressure at the brain. Account for the vertical hydrostatic gradient; with an arterial line, use an explicitly documented brain-level reference such as the external auditory meatus rather than silently treating heart-level MAP as brain-level MAP. ([APSF: blood pressure in the beach-chair position (2020 safety review)](https://www.apsf.org/article/why-worry-about-blood-pressure-during-surgery-in-the-beach-chair-position/))

- Avoid deliberate hypotension for surgical visualization in the beach-chair position. The APSF safety review favors pressure close to the patient’s awake baseline, with prompt correction of hypotension and attention to oxygenation, ventilation and head position. ([APSF: blood pressure in the beach-chair position (2020 safety review)](https://www.apsf.org/article/why-worry-about-blood-pressure-during-surgery-in-the-beach-chair-position/))

- This safety review does not establish a single validated brain-MAP threshold for every patient. Do not transplant a study-specific number or a routine supine MAP target into this setting without considering the measurement level, baseline pressure and cerebrovascular risk. ([APSF: blood pressure in the beach-chair position (2020 safety review)](https://www.apsf.org/article/why-worry-about-blood-pressure-during-surgery-in-the-beach-chair-position/))

### Irrigation extravasation & extubation reassessment

- Watch for neck, face or chest swelling and changes in airway pressure or compliance during shoulder arthroscopy. Draping should permit appropriate observation of the neck and nearby chest when feasible. ([Shoulder arthroscopy fluid extravasation: systematic review (2018)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5954585/))

- Examine for extravasation and airway compromise before extubation. If significant edema is present, consider continued intubation and monitored postoperative care until the airway can be managed safely; do not assume completion of surgery means the airway is ready. ([Shoulder arthroscopy fluid extravasation: systematic review (2018)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5954585/))

- If the airway is not already secured, progressive neck swelling can make later intubation difficult. Escalate early when swelling or respiratory compromise develops. ([Shoulder arthroscopy fluid extravasation: systematic review (2018)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5954585/))

- The systematic review is dominated by case reports and small series. Its reported resolution times, pump settings and interventions are not validated universal thresholds, and it cannot establish a routine complication rate. ([Shoulder arthroscopy fluid extravasation: systematic review (2018)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5954585/))

### Cementation: team preparation & vigilance

- Before femoral-canal instrumentation and cement insertion, identify heightened cardiopulmonary risk, agree team roles and ensure the surgeon’s warning is heard and acknowledged.
