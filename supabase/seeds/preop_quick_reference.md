# Luma Quick Reference: Pre-op Cardiovascular Assessment

## Review draft

Proposed app subtitle: **Adult noncardiac surgery | Based on the 2024 AHA/ACC multisociety guideline.** This is an original, condensed clinical preview for owner review, not published app content or a comprehensive replacement for the guideline.

The linked document addresses perioperative cardiovascular management, not every component of pre-anesthesia assessment. This reference therefore uses “assessment” rather than a blanket declaration of “clearance,” and preserves the distinction between recommended actions and options that are reasonable or may be considered ([2024 AHA/ACC guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001285)).

## When a separate medical clearance visit may not be needed

**No separate clearance visit does not mean no preoperative assessment.** ASA standards call for evaluation before anesthesia care, with modification and documentation under exceptional circumstances; a separate PCP or specialist consultation is a different service, selected according to the clinical situation ([ASA Basic Standards for Preanesthesia Care](https://www.asahq.org/~/media/Sites/ASAHQ/Files/Public/Resources/standards-guidelines/basic-standards-for-preanesthesia-care.pdf); [Merck Manual: Preoperative Evaluation](https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/special-subjects/care-of-the-surgical-patient/preoperative-evaluation)).

### Situations that may not require an additional clearance appointment

- **Emergency surgery:** Do not delay life- or limb-saving treatment to obtain an outpatient clearance appointment; perform the rapid assessment feasible, especially reviewing allergies, anticoagulants, bleeding history, and previous anesthetic complications ([Merck Manual](https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/special-subjects/care-of-the-surgical-patient/preoperative-evaluation)).
- **Healthy, asymptomatic patient having a low-risk procedure:** A separate medical consultation may be unnecessary when the existing surgical/anesthesia assessment identifies no issue requiring additional evaluation or optimization; treat this as an individualized decision, not a blanket exemption from assessment ([Merck Manual](https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/special-subjects/care-of-the-surgical-patient/preoperative-evaluation); [ASA preanesthesia standards](https://www.asahq.org/~/media/Sites/ASAHQ/Files/Public/Resources/standards-guidelines/basic-standards-for-preanesthesia-care.pdf)).
- **Minor superficial procedures:** Superficial surgery falls within low-risk procedural categories, supporting limited, indication-based cardiovascular testing rather than an automatic cardiac workup; low procedural risk alone does not establish a universal exemption from a separate visit ([VCMC institutional guideline](https://vcportal.ventura.org/VCHCA/Hospitals/Medical%20Staff%20Services/CPG/vcmc-pre-operative-cpg-4-2015.pdf); [2024 AHA/ACC guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001285)).
- **Routine cataract surgery:** SAMBA recommends no testing unless indicated independently of the procedure; this supports avoiding unnecessary testing, not declaring that all preoperative assessment or consultation is prohibited ([SAMBA cataract position statement](https://samba.memberclicks.net/assets/docs/SAMBA_Statements/Preoperative_Care_for_Cataract_Surgery__The.11.pdf)).
- **Low cardiovascular risk:** Routine stress testing is not recommended for low-risk patients or low-risk procedures, and routine ECG is not recommended for asymptomatic patients undergoing low-risk surgery; these are testing recommendations, not rules eliminating all evaluation ([AHA recommendations, slides 29–34](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).

### Findings that are not stand-alone exemptions

- **Age below 40 or 50:** Do not use a universal age cutoff to waive evaluation; the cited VCMC document uses age thresholds for selected tests, not an age-based exemption from preoperative assessment ([VCMC institutional guideline](https://vcportal.ventura.org/VCHCA/Hospitals/Medical%20Staff%20Services/CPG/vcmc-pre-operative-cpg-4-2015.pdf)).
- **No chronic disease or daily prescriptions:** These may support a lower-risk profile, but absence of diagnoses or medications is not a substitute for reviewing symptoms, medical/anesthetic history, and relevant examination findings ([ASA preanesthesia standards](https://www.asahq.org/~/media/Sites/ASAHQ/Files/Public/Resources/standards-guidelines/basic-standards-for-preanesthesia-care.pdf)).
- **Local anesthesia alone:** Do not present local anesthesia as an automatic exemption; assess the patient and procedure rather than assuming that the anesthetic label eliminates risk or consultation needs ([Merck Manual](https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/special-subjects/care-of-the-surgical-patient/preoperative-evaluation)).
- **Stable chronic disease:** Its presence does not automatically require a specialist “clearance”; seek consultation when it would help evaluate a relevant problem, optimize treatment, or change perioperative management ([Merck Manual](https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/special-subjects/care-of-the-surgical-patient/preoperative-evaluation)).

## At a glance: proceed, evaluate, or defer

- **Emergency surgery:** Perform the focused assessment and management feasible without delaying life- or limb-saving intervention; routine elective testing pathways do not take priority over emergency care ([AHA/ACC guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001285)).
- **Active cardiac condition:** Postpone elective surgery for evaluation and treatment of acute coronary syndrome, decompensated heart failure, or unstable arrhythmia ([guideline algorithm overview](https://www.ccjm.org/content/92/4/213)).
- **Low risk or adequate functional capacity:** Routine ischemia testing is not recommended when predicted perioperative risk is low, the procedure is low risk, or functional capacity is adequate with stable symptoms ([AHA recommendations, slides 32–34](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Elevated risk plus poor or unknown functional capacity:** Consider selective further evaluation only when findings could change management; this combination does not automatically require a stress test or cancellation ([AHA/ACC guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001285)).
- **Important risk modifier:** Address severe valve disease, pulmonary hypertension, congenital heart disease, prior PCI/CABG, recent stroke, a cardiac device, or frailty even when the calculated risk appears reassuring ([guideline algorithm overview](https://www.ccjm.org/content/92/4/213)).

## Establish surgical urgency

- **Emergency:** Immediate threat to life or limb; generally less than 2 hours available ([AHA/ACC guideline, section 1.5](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001285)).
- **Urgent:** Threat to life or limb; generally 2 to less than 24 hours available ([AHA/ACC guideline, section 1.5](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001285)).
- **Time-sensitive:** May be delayed up to 3 months for evaluation and management without adversely affecting outcomes ([AHA/ACC guideline, section 1.5](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001285)).
- **Elective:** Can be delayed to complete appropriate evaluation and treatment ([AHA/ACC guideline, section 1.5](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001285)).

## Identify symptoms and estimate risk

- **Symptoms requiring attention:** Chest pain, dyspnea, unexplained palpitations, tachycardia, syncope, or a murmur should inform targeted cardiovascular evaluation ([AHA recommendations, slide 29](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Risk estimate:** Combine patient and procedural factors using a validated tool, such as RCRI, MICA, or ACS NSQIP; the guideline does not require a single preferred calculator ([AHA/ACC guideline, section 3.1](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001285)).
- **Risk categories:** Low risk is predicted major adverse cardiovascular event risk below 1%; elevated risk is 1% or greater, interpreted using the chosen tool and its outcome definitions ([AHA/ACC guideline, section 1.5](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001285)).
- **Functional capacity:** Use structured assessment, such as the Duke Activity Status Index (DASI), particularly before elevated-risk surgery; less than 4 METs or DASI 34 or lower identifies poor capacity for the guideline’s testing pathway ([AHA recommendations, slides 22–23 and 32](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Frailty:** A validated frailty assessment can help guide care before elevated-risk surgery in older adults and younger patients with suspected frailty ([AHA recommendations, slide 24](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).

## Decide whether testing is indicated

### ECG

- **Reasonable before elevated-risk surgery:** Known coronary disease, significant arrhythmia, peripheral arterial disease, cerebrovascular disease, significant structural heart disease, or cardiovascular symptoms ([AHA recommendations, slide 29](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **New ECG abnormality:** Further evaluation is reasonable for findings such as new ST/T changes, pathologic Q waves, Mobitz II or higher AV block, bundle branch block, QT prolongation, or atrial fibrillation ([AHA recommendations, slide 30](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Asymptomatic patient without known CVD:** A baseline ECG may be considered for elevated-risk surgery, but routine ECG is not recommended for asymptomatic patients having low-risk procedures ([AHA recommendations, slide 30](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).

### Echocardiography / ventricular function

- **Obtain LV-function assessment:** New dyspnea, examination findings of heart failure, or suspected new or worsening ventricular dysfunction ([AHA recommendations, slide 31](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Reassess known heart failure when clinically changed:** Worsening dyspnea or another change in status supports repeat assessment; routine repeat LV-function testing is not recommended in asymptomatic, clinically stable patients ([AHA recommendations, slide 31](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Suspected valve disease:** Obtain preoperative echocardiography for suspected moderate/severe aortic stenosis before elevated-risk elective surgery and for suspected moderate/severe valvular regurgitation before elective surgery ([AHA recommendations, slides 56 and 59](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).

### Biomarkers

- **Who qualifies for consideration:** Elevated-risk surgery plus known CVD, age 65 or older, or age 45 or older with symptoms suggestive of CVD; BNP/NT-proBNP measurement is reasonable, and troponin measurement may be reasonable ([AHA recommendations, slide 27](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Algorithm thresholds:** BNP greater than 92 ng/L, NT-proBNP 300 ng/L or greater, or troponin above the assay’s 99th-percentile upper reference limit are abnormal thresholds used in the pathway, not stand-alone cancellation criteria ([AHA algorithm, slide 37](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Elevated result:** Discuss whether to proceed with an adapted plan, investigate further, or consider alternatives; biomarker elevation alone does not mandate stress testing or coronary intervention ([guideline algorithm overview](https://www.ccjm.org/content/92/4/213)).

### Stress testing, CCTA, and coronary intervention

- **Selective stress testing:** May be considered when elevated-risk surgery, elevated calculated cardiovascular risk, and poor/unknown functional capacity coexist, provided the result could change management ([AHA/ACC guideline, section 4.3](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001285)).
- **Selective coronary CT angiography:** May be considered in the same selected population to identify high-risk coronary anatomy; neither CCTA nor stress testing is routine for low-risk patients or those with adequate capacity and stable symptoms ([AHA recommendations, slides 32–34](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Avoid routine invasive angiography:** It is not recommended solely to improve perioperative outcomes ([AHA recommendations, slide 35](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Revascularization:** Treat acute coronary syndrome as appropriate and defer elective surgery; for hemodynamically significant left-main stenosis of at least 50%, revascularization and deferral are reasonable, whereas routine prophylactic revascularization for non-left-main disease is not recommended ([AHA recommendations, slide 39](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).

## Check timing after PCI or stroke

- **Balloon angioplasty without a stent:** Delay elective surgery at least 14 days ([AHA recommendations, slide 73](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Drug-eluting stent for acute coronary syndrome:** If elective surgery requires interruption of one or more antiplatelet agents, ideally delay at least 12 months ([AHA recommendations, slide 73](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Drug-eluting stent for chronic coronary disease:** If antiplatelet interruption is required, delaying elective surgery at least 6 months is reasonable ([AHA recommendations, slide 73](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Time-sensitive surgery after a drug-eluting stent:** May be considered at 3 months or later when antiplatelet interruption is necessary and the harm of delaying surgery exceeds the cardiovascular risk ([AHA recommendations, slide 74](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Recent stent danger window:** Elective surgery requiring antiplatelet interruption within 30 days of a bare-metal or drug-eluting stent is potentially harmful ([AHA recommendations, slide 74](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Stroke or TIA:** Delaying elective surgery at least 3 months after the most recent event is reasonable ([AHA recommendations, slide 66](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).

## Address valve disease and other high-risk conditions

- **Severe aortic stenosis:** Evaluate the need for valve intervention before elective surgery; asymptomatic moderate/severe AS with normal LV systolic function documented by echo within the past year can reasonably proceed to low-risk elective surgery ([AHA recommendations, slide 56](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Severe mitral stenosis:** Evaluate the need for valve intervention before elective surgery; when intervention is not possible beforehand, invasive monitoring is reasonable and perioperative heart-rate control may be considered ([AHA recommendations, slide 58](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Moderate/severe mitral regurgitation:** Elective surgery is reasonable when asymptomatic, LV systolic function is normal, and estimated pulmonary artery systolic pressure is below 50 mm Hg ([AHA recommendations, slide 59](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Moderate/severe aortic regurgitation:** Elective surgery is reasonable when asymptomatic with normal LV systolic function, specified as LVEF above 55%; patients meeting valve-intervention indications warrant consideration of intervention before elevated-risk elective surgery ([AHA recommendations, slide 59](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Compensated heart failure:** Continue guideline-directed therapy unless contraindicated, except for the preoperative SGLT2-inhibitor hold; new decompensation requires reassessment rather than routine progression through the elective pathway ([AHA recommendations, slide 42](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en); [algorithm overview](https://www.ccjm.org/content/92/4/213)).
- **Severe pulmonary hypertension:** Before elevated-risk surgery, specialized PH consultation and planning for monitoring/postoperative care are reasonable; continue established pulmonary arterial hypertension therapies ([AHA recommendations, slides 46–47](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Congenital heart disease or LVAD:** Intermediate/elevated-risk congenital lesions require adult congenital-heart specialist consultation before elective surgery; LVAD patients require coordination with their LVAD team ([AHA recommendations, slides 48 and 55](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy:** Continue established beta-blocker or nondihydropyridine calcium-channel-blocker therapy and plan to avoid tachycardia, reduced preload/afterload, and factors that worsen dynamic outflow obstruction ([AHA recommendations, slides 44–45](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Pacemaker/ICD:** If electromagnetic interference is anticipated, establish a device-specific plan before surgery, including device type, manufacturer/model, pacing dependence, known magnet response, and restoration of any altered functions ([AHA recommendations, slides 62–64](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).

## Review blood pressure and medications

- **Poorly controlled hypertension:** Deferral may be considered for elective elevated-risk surgery when cardiovascular risk factors coexist with a recent history of SBP at least 180 or DBP at least 110 mm Hg before the day of surgery; this is not a universal automatic cancellation threshold ([AHA recommendations, slide 40](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Beta blockers:** Continue stable therapy as clinically appropriate; if newly indicated, initiate sufficiently early, optimally more than 7 days before surgery, to assess tolerance and titrate, rather than starting on the day of surgery without an immediate indication ([AHA recommendations, slide 87](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Statins:** Continue existing therapy; initiate when independently indicated by ASCVD history or risk assessment, with intention for long-term use ([AHA recommendations, slide 69](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **ACE inhibitors/ARBs and other RAAS inhibitors:** For selected patients taking them for controlled hypertension before elevated-risk surgery, omission 24 hours beforehand may reduce hypotension; continuation is reasonable when prescribed for HFrEF ([AHA recommendations, slide 70](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **SGLT2 inhibitors:** Stop canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, and empagliflozin at least 3 days, and ertugliflozin at least 4 days, before scheduled surgery to reduce perioperative metabolic-acidosis risk ([AHA recommendations, slides 42 and 88](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Clonidine:** Do not newly initiate low-dose clonidine solely to reduce perioperative cardiovascular risk ([AHA recommendations, slide 71](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).

## GLP-1 / dual GIP–GLP-1 medications: aspiration-risk assessment

This is a separately sourced anesthesia addendum, not a recommendation attributed solely to the cardiovascular guideline. The ASA-led 2024 multisociety guidance and the 2025 SPAQI consensus differ in their dietary recommendations, so their pathways are labeled rather than combined ([multisociety guidance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11666732/); [SPAQI consensus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12597468/)).

### Identify the medication and screen for risk

- **Medication reconciliation:** Identify the agent, formulation, daily/weekly schedule, dose, last administration, indication, recent initiation or dose increases, and gastrointestinal symptoms; include semaglutide, liraglutide, dulaglutide, and dual GIP/GLP-1 agent tirzepatide, which is included in SPAQI’s medication review ([multisociety guidance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11666732/); [SPAQI consensus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12597468/)).
- **Risk factors:** Dose escalation, higher doses, weekly formulations, nausea/vomiting, abdominal pain, dyspepsia, constipation, and other conditions causing delayed emptying or dysmotility warrant attention under the multisociety framework ([multisociety guidance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11666732/)).
- **Do not rely on symptoms alone:** Absence of gastrointestinal symptoms does not guarantee normal gastric emptying or an empty stomach ([SPAQI consensus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12597468/)).

### Continuation and risk mitigation: ASA-led multisociety approach

- **Lower-risk patients:** Therapy may continue before elective procedures when elevated risk of delayed gastric emptying and aspiration is not identified; routine discontinuation for every patient is not the default ([multisociety guidance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11666732/)).
- **Higher-risk patients:** Consider a liquid-only diet for at least 24 hours before the procedure, an aspiration-focused anesthesia plan, and day-of-procedure gastric ultrasound when available and performed by appropriately trained clinicians ([multisociety guidance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11666732/)).
- **Suspected or confirmed retained contents:** Discuss postponement versus proceeding with an appropriately modified anesthetic, including consideration of rapid-sequence induction with tracheal intubation when general anesthesia is appropriate; taking a GLP-1 drug is not itself an automatic cancellation rule ([multisociety guidance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11666732/)).
- **If a medication hold is selected:** The optimal interruption duration is unknown; the multisociety document suggests the earlier ASA intervals of holding daily agents on the procedure day and weekly agents for 1 week when the team determines a hold is necessary, not as a universal instruction or guarantee of an empty stomach ([multisociety guidance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11666732/)).
- **Diabetes considerations:** Balance interruption against hyperglycemia and loss of treatment benefit, and coordinate any alternative treatment with the prescribing team rather than automatically substituting insulin ([multisociety guidance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11666732/)).

### Separate guidance note: SPAQI 2025 fasting approach

- **Medication continuation:** Continue perioperatively in patients without significant gastrointestinal symptoms; SPAQI defines significant symptoms as severe nausea, vomiting, or inability to tolerate oral intake, not fullness or early satiety alone ([SPAQI consensus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12597468/)).
- **Solid food:** For these patients, fast from solids for 24 hours, allowing only clear liquids during that period until the applicable liquid cutoff ([SPAQI consensus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12597468/)).
- **High-carbohydrate clear liquids:** Fast for 8 hours when glucose content is 10% or greater ([SPAQI consensus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12597468/)).
- **No-/low-carbohydrate clear liquids:** Fast for 4 hours when glucose content is below 10%, including water ([SPAQI consensus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12597468/)).
- **Significant symptoms:** Refer for prescriber-directed diet and medication modification before elective anesthesia; severely symptomatic patients should not proceed with elective procedures ([SPAQI consensus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12597468/)).
- **Glucose safety during dietary restriction:** Patients with diabetes need an individualized glucose-monitoring and medication plan during the clear-liquid period ([SPAQI consensus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12597468/)).

### Urgent/emergency procedures and app safety wording

- **Urgent/emergency surgery:** The ASA’s 2023 emergency recommendation is to proceed with full-stomach precautions rather than wait for a medication hold interval; distinguish this emergency precaution from the older blanket elective withholding approach ([ASA 2023 statement, reproduced PDF](https://www.swa10.com/uploads/6/1/4/3/61438899/glp-1.asa.2023.pdf)).
- **Diet is not the same as fasting:** “Liquid-only for 24 hours” does not mean liquids may continue until anesthesia; the 2024 multisociety document does not specify the same detailed liquid cutoffs as SPAQI, so confirm the institution’s adopted diet and fasting instructions ([multisociety guidance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11666732/); [SPAQI consensus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12597468/)).

Proposed app banner: **Do not automatically stop every GLP-1 medication or assume that a 7-day hold eliminates aspiration risk. Confirm the patient-specific plan and the facility’s adopted fasting protocol.**

## Confirm the antithrombotic plan

- **Prior PCI:** Continue aspirin 75–100 mg if possible, with multidisciplinary assessment of bleeding and thrombosis risk ([AHA recommendations, slides 72–74](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Early surgery after stenting:** For time-sensitive surgery within 30 days of a bare-metal stent or less than 3 months after a drug-eluting stent, continue dual antiplatelet therapy unless bleeding risk outweighs protection against stent thrombosis ([AHA recommendations, slide 74](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **If interruption has been selected:** The guideline lists platelet-function recovery intervals of aspirin 4 days, clopidogrel 5–7 days, prasugrel 7–10 days, and ticagrelor 3–5 days; these are not instructions to stop every patient’s therapy ([AHA recommendations, slide 76](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Oral anticoagulants:** Base interruption on the agent, renal clearance, bleeding risk, and thrombotic risk; routine heparin bridging is not recommended for most patients, while selected high-thrombotic-risk patients interrupting a vitamin K antagonist may benefit ([AHA recommendations, slides 78–84](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Neuraxial planning:** Do not treat general surgical interruption intervals as stand-alone authorization for neuraxial procedures; the guideline specifically calls for additional team-based consideration where bleeding consequences are high, including neuraxial anesthesia ([AHA recommendations, slide 82](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).

## Other preoperative items covered in this guideline

- **Diabetes:** HbA1c testing is reasonable in patients with diabetes or at risk for diabetes if no result is available within the preceding 3 months ([AHA recommendations, slide 88](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Metformin: guidelines differ.** The 2024 AHA/ACC cardiovascular guideline considers perioperative continuation reasonable, whereas ADA 2026 recommends holding metformin and other oral glucose-lowering agents on the day of surgery/procedure. Follow the institution's adopted diabetes medication plan and assess renal function/acute illness; do not interpret the cardiovascular recommendation as a universal instruction to continue. See **Pre-Op Glucose** for the ADA-based chart ([AHA recommendations, slide 88](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en); [ADA 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12690180/)).
- **OSA:** Screening with a validated questionnaire is reasonable to assess perioperative complication risk ([AHA recommendations, slide 67](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).
- **Iron-deficiency anemia:** Preoperative oral or IV iron is reasonable before elective surgery to increase hemoglobin and reduce transfusion requirements ([AHA recommendations, slide 96](https://professional.heart.org/-/media/PHD-Files-2/Science-News/2/2024/2024-Guideline-for-Perioperative-Cardiovascular-Management-slide-set.pdf?sc_lang=en)).

## Proposed app closing block

These are proposed documentation prompts, not a validated clearance score. The reference would end with the following concise bullets:

- **Assessment:** Procedure/urgency, symptoms, calculated risk, functional capacity, and important risk modifiers.
- **Outstanding issues:** Additional testing or consultation, who will review results, and what findings would change the plan.
- **Medication/device plan:** Specific instructions, antithrombotic coordination, and any pacemaker/ICD arrangements.
- **Disposition:** Proceed with the agreed plan, obtain further evaluation, or defer elective surgery for optimization.
- **Communication:** Document the team’s risk-benefit discussion and the patient’s preferences.

These documentation prompts synthesize the assessment and team-planning principles in the [AHA/ACC perioperative guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001285) and [ASA preanesthesia standards](https://www.asahq.org/~/media/Sites/ASAHQ/Files/Public/Resources/standards-guidelines/basic-standards-for-preanesthesia-care.pdf); they are not a quoted or validated clearance checklist.
